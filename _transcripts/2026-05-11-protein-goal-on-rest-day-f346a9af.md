### CLAUDE (2026-05-12T00:39)
 You're right to push back on that — I overstated it. The current consensus has moved away from the strict "20–40g per meal cap" model.

The older view came from acute studies measuring muscle protein synthesis (MPS) response in the 3–5 hours after a meal, which showed MPS plateauing around 20–25g of high-quality protein. But more recent research — particularly Trommelen et al. (2023) using extended tracer studies out to 12 hours — found that larger doses (100g in their study) continued to drive whole-body protein anabolism well beyond the early MPS window. The body doesn't just "waste" excess protein via oxidation; a meaningful fraction gets stored and used over a longer timeframe, including for non-muscle protein turnover, gut tissue, immune function, and delayed MPS.

For your situation specifically, this means: 59g protein at breakfast isn't suboptimal. It's actually well-suited to a few realities of your setup:
