### CLAUDE (2026-09-02T12:12)
This is a Facebook ad for a compounded topical combining dutasteride, minoxidil, and cetirizine. Quick take on the actual evidence:

**What's legit:**
- **Minoxidil** — solid evidence for slowing loss/promoting regrowth, topical form is FDA-approved (Rogaine)
- **Dutasteride** — a 5-alpha reductase inhibitor like finasteride, but stronger. Oral dutasteride has good trial data for hair loss; topical/compounded versions have much thinner evidence, though some studies suggest it works with fewer systemic side effects than oral
- **Cetirizine** (antihistamine) added topically for scalp inflammation — this one's the weakest link. It's mostly there because some small studies link antihistamines to hair growth via a different pathway, but it's not a well-established treatment on its own

**Red flags on this specific offer:**
- "Top 5 treatments reviewed" content marketing → funnel to one specific compounded product is a common affiliate/ad pattern, not necessarily an independent review
- Compounded combo products aren't FDA-approved as a combination — efficacy of the combo isn't the same as efficacy of the individual ingredients
- No dosage/concentration info shown, which matters a lot for both efficacy and side effects (especially dutasteride, which has real ones — libido/sexual side effects, category X for pregnancy exposure)

**Bottom line:** the individual active ingredients (especially minoxidil and dutasteride) have real evidence behind them for hair loss. Whether *this specific compounded product* delivers effective concentrations, and whether the ad's claims reflect independent testing vs marketing copy, isn't something I can verify from the image. I'd be skeptical of the "team reviewed" framing and would want to see actual formulation details before trusting it over talking to a dermatologist about oral/topical finasteride or dutasteride directly.
