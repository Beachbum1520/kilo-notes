### CLAUDE (2026-09-18T13:12)

Yes — expense it, don't capitalize.

**Why:** This is a repair (glow plug + engine labor), not a betterment/adaptation/restoration under the IRS's capitalization regs (Reg. §1.263(a)-3, the "BAR" test). A glow plug replacement keeps the truck in normal operating condition — it doesn't extend useful life, increase value, or adapt it to a new use. That makes it a fully deductible Sec. 162 repair expense in the year paid, on Schedule F, regardless of the dollar amount. The de minimis safe harbor ($2,500/item cap without an AFS) doesn't even apply here — that threshold governs discrete asset purchases, not repairs.

One flag worth your own follow-up, not a tax issue: this truck (2018 K-series 3500 Duramax, VIN …201117) is invoiced to you personally, paid from USAA personal account per the handwritten note — separate question from deductibility, but worth confirming this is the truck you've got farm-use classified with USAA (that file's on the 2500, this invoice's model field is ambiguous/generic "1500-3500"), since farm-use vs. personal-use classification is the same logic gate for whether it belongs on Schedule F at all.

Separately, not a tax question but worth a glance: $5,000 labor for an 8-hour glow plug job ($40/hr labor rate shown, yet billed as a flat $5,000 "Misc/Engine Labor" line) is a striking rate — worth a quick call to Dusty's if you didn't already discuss it.
