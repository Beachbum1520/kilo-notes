### CLAUDE (2026-04-28T14:25)
 For a Bond Arms Snake Slayer at typical snake-killing distances (5-15 feet), **#8 is the better choice** for most situations.

Here's the tradeoff:

**#9 shot** — smaller pellets, more of them per shell. Tighter, denser pattern at very close range. Better for small snakes at 3-8 feet. Less penetration, so less risk of ricochet off rocks/hard ground.

**#8 shot** — slightly larger pellets, better penetration through a snake's body, holds pattern integrity a bit better at 10-15 feet. More forgiving on shot placement because each pellet hits harder.

Given you're dealing with cottonmouths (thick-bodied, tough scales compared to a rattler), #8 gives you better penetration to actually put the snake down rather than just peppering it. Cottonmouths can take a hit and keep coming if you don't hit vitals or break the spine.

Practical recommendation: **Federal Premium Handgun .410 #8 shot** or **Winchester Super-X .410 #8** are solid, widely available loads. Some folks on the farm circuit run #6 in the Snake Slayer for extra penetration on bigger cottonmouths, but pattern density drops noticeably — #8 is the sweet spot.

One note: the Snake Slayer's 3.5" barrel patterns wider than a longer shotgun, so keep shots inside 12-15 feet for reliable kills. Past that you're throwing a sparse cloud of shot.

### CLAUDE (2026-04-29T23:32)
 Turkey loads for snake control on the farm — let's pressure-test this before you click "buy."

**The case for turkey shot:**
Turkey loads are typically #4, #5, or #6 in heavy payloads (often with copper plating or buffered shot for tight patterns at 40+ yards). Big pellets, lots of energy per pellet. A #5 or #6 turkey load would absolutely flatten a cottonmouth.

**The case against — and it's strong for your use case:**

1. **Pattern is too tight at snake distance.** Turkey loads are engineered to hold a dense pattern at 40 yards through a tight choke. Out of a 3.5" Snake Slayer barrel at 5-10 feet, you're throwing a fist-sized cluster of shot. Miss by 6 inches and the snake is fine. The whole point of #8/#9 in a derringer is the wide, forgiving spread at close range.

2. **Overpenetration and ricochet.** Big copper-plated pellets at 1200 fps will skip off rocks, hard-packed dirt, fence posts, and concrete pad areas around the barn. You're often shooting downward at a snake — that's the worst angle for ricochet. Small shot dumps energy into the snake and dirt; large shot keeps going.

3. **Cost and availability for a problem already solved.** Turkey loads run $2-4 per round. You're paying premium ammo prices for worse terminal effect on snakes than the $0.70/round Winchester AA #8 sitting on the shelf 30 minutes from you.

4. **3" chamber question still applies.** Most quality turkey loads are 3" — confirm the Bond Arms model first.

**Where turkey shot would make sense:**
If you were dealing with feral hogs at close range, a coyote prowling the chicken coop, or needed a defensive load for two-legged threats. The Bond Arms .410/.45 Colt platform is actually marketed with that flexibility in mind — many owners load one chamber with a snake/birdshot round and the other with a defensive load (Federal Handgun .410 with 4 pellets of 000 buck, or just .45 Colt).

**My recommendation:**
Buy the Winchester AA Sporting Clays #8 today for snake duty. If you want a second load for the other chamber for general farm defense (hogs, coyotes, two-legged threats), look at **Federal Premium Personal Defense .410 Handgun (4 pellets 000 buck + disc) or just run .45 Colt** — that's where the Bond Arms platform earns its keep.

Don't overthink the snake load. Small shot, close range, dead snake.
