### CLAUDE (2026-09-27T16:38)

**Not a waste, but don't pay full price, and it won't get you conversational alone.** Pimsleur's Tagalog course only goes through Levels 1–2, which is 60 half-hour lessons. That's a solid beginner foundation (pronunciation, sentence structure, basic exchanges), then it stops. Two other caveats:

- **It's free at many libraries.** Library systems carry the Tagalog lessons through OverDrive, so check Libby with your Troup County card before you buy anything.
- **It teaches textbook Tagalog.** Real speech in Manila is heavy Taglish. Pimsleur won't prepare you for how people actually talk.

**What actually gets you conversational:**

1. **A Filipino tutor on italki or Preply.** This is the biggest lever by far. Tutors often run $8–15/hr. Book 3 sessions a week and tell them you want Taglish conversation, not grammar drills.
2. **Pimsleur alongside the tutor** for the first 2–3 months, during commute and runs. Audio-only suits your schedule.
3. **Your call center.** You have 100+ native speakers. Ask a couple of team leads to speak Tagalog with you in casual moments. It's free, it's daily, and it has a side benefit for rapport.
4. **Skip the apps** (Ling, Drops, etc.). They build vocabulary but don't produce conversation.

**Realistic timeline:** with a tutor 3x/week plus daily audio, expect basic conversation in about 6 months. With Pimsleur alone, you'll get polite phrases and be lost the moment someone answers at normal speed.

### SCOTT (2026-09-27T16:40)
I already have the paid version of pimsluer . You really think 3 tutors a week?  I was thinking pimsluer and maybe one tutor session per week?

### CLAUDE (2026-09-27T16:40)
One a week works, but it roughly doubles the timeline, to 12+ months for basic conversation instead of about 6. The problem isn't total hours. With a 7-day gap, you spend the first chunk of each session recovering what you forgot. Conversation is a speaking skill, and Pimsleur only gets you partway: you respond to prompts, but you never have to produce your own sentences under pressure.

**If you go with one session a week, make it count:**
- **Use 60 minutes, not 30.** Warm-up eats the first 10–15.
- **Make it pure conversation.** Pimsleur covers structure, so the tutor's job is making you talk.
- **Fill the gap with live speaking.** Two or three short Tagalog exchanges a week with your team, even 5 minutes on Slack voice or a call, close most of the gap between one and three sessions.
- **Record each session** and replay it on runs alongside Pimsleur.

**A middle option:** two 30-minute sessions a week. That's the same cost and time as one hour, but the shorter gap does most of the retention work of three sessions.

So Pimsleur plus one weekly session is fine if you're patient. If you have a date in mind, like a trip where you want to hold real conversations, go with two.

Ok. Two it is. How many of the Pimsleur sessions should I do before the tutor?

### CLAUDE (2026-09-27T16:41)
**About 10 lessons, so roughly 2 weeks at one a day.** Don't wait longer than that.

By lesson 10 you'll have greetings, basic questions, numbers, and simple sentences. That's enough for the tutor to run the session mostly in Tagalog instead of explaining in English. Waiting until the end of Level 1 (lesson 30) doesn't buy much. The tutor's value is forcing you to speak, and the earlier that starts, the less you rely on recognition-only habits.

After that, run both in parallel: one Pimsleur lesson a day, with tutor sessions twice a week. Tell the tutor which lesson you're on so they can reinforce that material instead of jumping ahead.

### SCOTT (2026-09-27T16:45)
I have done the Pimsleur lesson one many many times. When I am doing the lesson I feel like I get it but as soon as the lesson is over I feel like I have lost it. Should I keep this approach or just power through the lessons?

### CLAUDE (2026-09-27T16:45)
**Power through.** Repeating lesson 1 works against how Pimsleur is built.

The course relies on spaced recall. Material from lesson 1 comes back in lessons 2, 3, 5, 8 and beyond, at growing intervals. The forgetting between reviews is part of the design, because recalling something just as it's slipping is what locks it in. Replaying lesson 1 skips that and gives you short-term familiarity that fades an hour later, which matches what you're describing.

That "I get it in the lesson, then it's gone" feeling is normal. During the lesson you're prompted, so you're recognizing. Afterward nothing prompts you, so you'd have to recall on your own, and that skill only builds through the spaced repetition in later lessons.

**Rule of thumb:** move on if you answer about 80% of the prompts correctly. Only repeat a lesson if you're missing more than that.

**One habit that helps:** right after each lesson, take 2 minutes with no audio and say out loud the 5–6 phrases you remember. That's active recall, and it does more than a second listen.
