### CLAUDE (2026-05-18T01:27)
 Mechanically, yes — sealed plastic soda bottles aren't on any prohibited list, and people do check beverages. But a few things worth weighing before you do it:

Weight: a 12-pack of 12 oz bottles is around 10–11 lbs. If your bag is already near the 50 lb limit, that eats a big chunk and overage fees are steep ($100+ on most carriers).

Pressure and handling: checked holds are pressurized but temperatures swing, and bags get thrown. Carbonated bottles can leak around the cap or, occasionally, split a seam. Double-bagging in trash bags or wrapping in clothes is smart insurance — a burst Dr Pepper through your whole suitcase is a bad day.

Cost math: a 12-pack runs maybe $7–9 at a grocery store at your destination. Unless you're going somewhere it's genuinely hard to find, packing it is usually more hassle than it's worth.

Where are you headed? If it's the Philippines, Diet Dr Pepper is genuinely hard to find there and the calculus changes — that's one of the few cases where packing it actually makes sense.

### CLAUDE (2026-05-18T01:28)
 That changes the math considerably. 70 lb allowance plus a real scarcity at the destination — pack it.

A few tips from people who've done this run:

Pressure-proof the cans/bottles. Put the 12-pack inside two trash bags, twist and tie each separately. Even one leaker in a sealed bag stays contained.

Pad the center of your suitcase with the 12-pack and surround it with clothes — keeps it from shifting and adds a buffer against rough handling.

Consider splitting across two bags if you're checking two. Business class usually gets 2x70 lb on international, and spreading the weight makes lifting easier and reduces single-point-of-failure risk if a bag gets delayed or lost.

Worth knowing: Diet Dr Pepper has aspartame, which is heat-sensitive. If your bag sits on hot tarmac in Manila or a layover hub, flavor can degrade a bit on a long haul. Not dangerous, just slightly flatter taste. Not a reason to skip it — just don't expect it to taste freezer-fresh after 20+ hours of transit.

If you want more cans per pound, the 7.5 oz mini cans give you more individual servings for less weight per serving, though Diet Dr Pepper minis can be hit or miss to find. Standard 12 oz is the safe bet.
