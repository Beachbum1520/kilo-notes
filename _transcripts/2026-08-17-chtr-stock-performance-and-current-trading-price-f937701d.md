### CLAUDE (2026-08-17T19:29)

**First, nail down which instrument it is — the answer flips entirely.**

- **RSUs (restricted stock units):** you get shares outright at vest. Worth something at any price. Taxed as ordinary income on the full value at vest.
- **Stock options (NQSOs/ISOs):** you get the *right to buy* at a strike price. If CHTR sits below strike at expiry, they're worth zero.

"Restricted stock options" isn't a real instrument — it's almost certainly one or the other, and Charter's proxy will say. Get the grant docs before you build any plan.

**If they're RSUs, the framing most people get wrong:**

Holding after vest is *not* a tax deferral. You're taxed on the full vest-date value as ordinary income either way. So the real question is: *if someone handed you that amount in cash on vest day, would you buy CHTR with all of it?* Selling at vest is the tax-neutral default; holding is an affirmative decision to concentrate.

**If they're options:** CHTR is a highly levered equity — roughly $90B of debt sitting under a ~$20B market cap. That means the equity is a thin slice, and options on it are leverage on leverage. Small EBITDA swings move the stock violently in both directions. A grant struck near current lows has real upside if the subscriber bleed stabilizes; it goes to zero if it doesn't.

**The concentration argument specific to you:**

Your salary, your LTIP, your career trajectory, and now your equity all sit on the same balance sheet — and your job risk is *positively* correlated with the stock. If CHTR underperforms, you get cost-cutting, onshoring pressure, and BPRF divestiture chatter at the same moment your equity is down. That's not diversification, that's a leveraged bet on one employer.

**Two things to run down before the grants land:**

1. **Forfeiture terms on departure.** Same open item you already have on the Cox awards. If BPRF gets divested or the in-office mandate pushes you out, unvested equity likely walks. That argues for taking liquidity at each vest rather than accumulating a pile you may never own.
2. **Trading windows.** At your level you may be a designated insider with blackout periods. A 10b5-1 plan set up in an open window is the standard mechanism for selling on a schedule without timing questions.

I'm not a financial advisor, and equity comp interacts with your full tax and retirement picture — worth an hour with a CPA once you have the actual grant terms in hand.

### CLAUDE (2026-08-17T19:30)

Then the default is **sell at vest**, and holding needs an affirmative reason. Here's the reasoning:

**The tax math removes the usual excuse to hold.** RSUs are taxed as ordinary income on the full vest-date value whether you sell or not. Your cost basis resets to the vest price. So selling immediately generates essentially zero additional tax — the gain-or-loss clock starts at zero. Holding buys you nothing tax-wise; it just exposes you to CHTR price risk on money you've already paid income tax on.

Watch one trap: most plans withhold at a flat 22% supplemental rate. At your income that's under-withholding, and you'll owe the difference in April. Set aside the gap on every vest.

**The concentration case is stronger for you than for a typical employee.** Your comp, your job, and your equity all key off the same broadband subscriber trend. If CHTR keeps bleeding subs, you get the equity drawdown *and* the cost-cutting pressure *and* the onshoring mandate simultaneously. Holding RSUs stacks a third bet on top of two you can't unwind.

**What would justify holding:**
- You'd buy CHTR with cash today at that price, on the merits — the 4x P/E and FCF story is a real bull case, not a stupid one.
- You want it, but you'd size it as a normal position (say 5–10% of liquid net worth), not "however much happens to vest."
- Long-term capital gains treatment on appreciation *above* vest price — but that's a reason to hold a position you'd own anyway, not a reason to acquire one.

**Concrete next steps:**
1. Read the grant agreement for the vest schedule and the forfeiture-on-termination language — same open item you've got on the Cox awards. If BPRF gets divested or you leave, unvested shares likely walk.

3. Decide your target CHTR allocation *now*, before the first vest, in writing. Sell everything above it. Deciding in the moment is how people end up 60% concentrated in their employer.

Not financial advice — the vest schedule and your overall tax picture matter, and this is worth an hour with your CPA once you have the grant docs.
