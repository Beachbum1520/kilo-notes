 Great — let's talk box photography.

Here's the strategic question first: **how do you photograph a "box" that's actually 5–9 individual vacuum-sealed packages?**

You have three real options, and they each tell a different story:

---

**Option 1: The Spread Shot**

Lay all the contents out flat on the marble counter, fanned out so customers can see every package. Same kitchen, same skillet, same towel formula you used for the brats and bacon.

- ✅ Customer sees exactly what they're getting
- ✅ Looks like a lot of food (good for value perception)
- ✅ Same visual language as your other product photos
- ❌ Requires 5–6 different photo sessions (one per box)
- ❌ A box with 9 packages can look cluttered

---

**Option 2: The Open Box Shot**

Use one of your actual 12×12 shipping boxes, partially fill with vacuum-sealed packages, and shoot from a 45° angle showing the open box.

- ✅ Tells the "unboxing" story — exactly what arrives at the customer's door
- ✅ Premium / curated feel — like ButcherBox / Crowd Cow / Porter Road
- ✅ One consistent visual treatment across all 5 boxes
- ❌ Need to source/stage an unused box and liner
- ❌ Lighting is harder inside a box
- ❌ Customer can't see what's inside as clearly

---

**Option 3: The Stylized Hero**

Plate one cooked item from the box (e.g., grilled brats for Grill Master, smoked ribs for Slow Cook) as the hero, with vacuum-sealed packages stacked behind.

- ✅ Sells the meal, not the package
- ✅ Most aspirational / professional look
- ❌ Requires you to actually cook each one
- ❌ Slower workflow
- ❌ You already said you're not cooking today

---

**My recommendation: Option 2 (Open Box Shot) for all 5 boxes.**

Here's why:

1. **Visual consistency.** One photo style across all 5 box SKUs makes the lineup look intentional and professional. Like a real product line, not a random assortment.

2. **Unboxing is the story.** The whole point of a curated box is the experience of opening it. Showing that on the product page sells the experience, not just the contents.

3. **You can shoot all 5 in one session.** Set up one staging area with your shipping box, liner, and a few props. Swap the contents between shots. Done in 30 minutes.

4. **It separates boxes from your individual SKUs visually.** Customers scanning your store will instantly know "this is a curated box, not a single product."

5. **It hides minor imperfections.** Vacuum seal frost, label inconsistencies, weird package shapes — all less visible when the box is the hero.

---

**If you go with Option 2, here's the setup:**

- 1× clean 12×12 shipping box
- 1× clean liner (or rolled back so we see meat, not insulation)
- Same marble counter or wood cutting board surface you used before
- Maybe the cast iron skillet next to the box for visual continuity
- Maybe the gray check towel draped over the corner of the box

Then for each box, just swap the contents inside. Photograph from a slight angle so the camera looks DOWN into the box and you see the packages arranged inside.
