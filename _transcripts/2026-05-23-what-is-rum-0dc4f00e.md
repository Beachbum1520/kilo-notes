### CLAUDE (2026-05-23T11:01)
 Rum is a distilled alcoholic spirit made by fermenting and distilling sugarcane byproducts — primarily molasses (the thick residue left after refining sugar) or fresh sugarcane juice.

**The basics:**
- **Origin:** Caribbean, 17th century, closely tied to the sugarcane plantation economy
- **Base ingredient:** Molasses (most common) or fresh cane juice (used in French-style *rhum agricole*)
- **Production:** Fermented, distilled, then typically aged in oak barrels
- **ABV:** Usually 40–50%, though overproof rums can exceed 75%

**Main styles:**
- **White/Silver** – Light, unaged or briefly aged, filtered clear. Common in cocktails (daiquiris, mojitos)
- **Gold/Aged** – Barrel-aged, richer flavor with caramel and vanilla notes
- **Dark** – Heavily aged or colored with molasses/caramel, bold and robust
- **Spiced** – Infused with spices like cinnamon, vanilla, or clove
- **Rhum Agricole** – French Caribbean style made from fresh cane juice, grassy and funky
- **Overproof** – High-alcohol rums (Jamaican style especially), used for depth in cocktails

**Key producing regions:** Jamaica, Barbados, Cuba, Puerto Rico, Martinique, Guyana, Guatemal, and the Dominican Republic — each with distinct flavor profiles driven by climate, yeast strains, and aging traditions.
