GAB stands for Graded Aggregate Base, which is Georgia's GDOT Section 815 specification for a dense-graded crushed stone with fines—essentially what most people call crusher run. It typically ranges from 1.5" to 2" down to dust, with fines passing the #200 sieve at around 5-12%. So crusher run and GAB are the same thing in Georgia.**GAB = Graded Aggregate Base.** It's a GDOT spec (Section 815), not a stone number like #57 or #4.

Dense-graded crushed stone — top size around 1½"–2" down through every size to dust. The fines are the point: they lock the stone together so it compacts tight instead of shifting like clean stone would.

What you're calling crusher run is the same material. At Georgia quarries "crusher run," "GAB," and "graded aggregate base" all pull from the same pile. If you want it exact, ask the quarry for GDOT 815 GAB and you'll get what Andrew is asking for.

Two things about this application specifically:

The seat is tapered — the photo shows a funnel narrowing from the 2" mouth to the throat. An expansion plug seals on straight bore. Push it past the taper into the parallel section before you tighten, or the rubber will just wedge in the cone and back out under head pressure.
