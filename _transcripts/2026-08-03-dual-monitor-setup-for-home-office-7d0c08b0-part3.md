# The real fix if you want the laptop screen too

**Computer glasses** — single-vision lenses ground for intermediate distance instead of the ~16" your readers use.

Do this before you go: sit at your desk in your normal posture and **measure eye-to-screen with a tape.** Give the optometrist that number. It's the detail people skip, and it's the difference between glasses that work and another pair that doesn't.

If you want one pair covering the laptop *and* the monitors, ask about **occupational lenses** — Zeiss Officelens, Varilux Computer, Essilor Eyezen. They're built with a wide intermediate zone plus near, specifically for desk work at two distances. Different animal from standard progressives, which give you a narrow intermediate corridor.

# One more thing from your photo

That monitor is mounted high. Top of the screen should sit at or just below eye level — you want to look slightly *down* at it, not up. A high monitor forces you to tilt your head back, which puts you through the wrong part of any lens and strains your neck.

Drop it a few inches on the arm while you're adjusting the distance. Free, and it may do more than you'd expect.
