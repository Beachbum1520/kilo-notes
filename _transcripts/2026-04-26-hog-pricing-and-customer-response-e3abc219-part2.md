ks** (1" thick), not whole hams. Far easier to sell 1-lb ham steaks than 8-lb roasts. Maybe keep one small whole ham per pig for holiday buyers.

**From the picnic shoulder:**
- **Grind it** or turn into **sausage** (mild Italian, breakfast, or hot)
- Don't bother with whole picnic — almost nobody buys those

**Trim, fat, scraps:**
- Grind into ground pork (most flexible)
- OR specialty sausages: bratwurst (original, cheddar, jalapeño-cheddar), Italian, breakfast
- Skip: leaf fat (unless you render lard for sale), liver, heart, kidney unless you have specific buyers

**Keep but minimize:**
- Hocks: 2-4 per pig for the soup-bean crowd
- Bacon ends: don't ask for these specifically — they happen naturally and are cheaper bacon

## How many boxes can you reasonably build?

A few box format options:

Each box: 3 lb bacon, 2 lb ham steak, 2 lb chops, 4 lb ground, 4 lb brats, 2 lb sausage, 1 lb tenderloin (split across boxes), 2 lb specialty (ribs/butt rotates)

**Yield: ~17-18 boxes per 2 pigs**
Revenue: 17 × $200 = **$3,400**

Mix of formats lets you hit different price points and use up inventory more flexibly.

**Yield: ~8 starter boxes ($920) + 9 premium boxes ($2,475) = $3,395**

More work to build but higher margin and stronger branding.

2 hogs at 225 lb HW × $5.50 = **$2,475 revenue**

## What I'd actually recommend

**Start simple with 1-2 SKUs.** Don't try to do 5 box types your first time. Pick:
1. A "Starter Box" (10 lbs, ~$110) — bacon, ground, brats, chops
2. A "Family Box" (20-25 lbs, ~$225-275) — everything in starter plus tenderloin, ribs, ham steaks

**Per 2 pigs, you should reasonably get 15-18 total boxes** depending on mix and exact weights.

**Pre-sell before processing.** The big win on boxes vs. wholes/halves is you can take orders and deposits *before* the pig is at the processor. Email everyone who inquired about wholes/halves, post the box options on Facebook, take orders for 2-3 weeks, then send the cut sheet to Resaca based on what you actually sold. No speculative inventory.

**Avoid:**
- Whole hams (do steaks instead)
- Whole picnic shoulder (grind it)
- Organ meats in boxes (sell separately to specific buyers if at all)
- Leaf fat in boxes (render or sell to charcuterie folks)
- Bone-in roasts that aren't Boston butt (most won't sell)

**Don't be afraid of grinding.** Every cut you grind into ground pork or sausage becomes more valuable per pound than it would be as a slow-moving roast. A $4/lb wholesale shoulder becomes $10/lb retail brats.
