### CLAUDE (2026-04-10T19:39)

Your frustration with Fitbod is pretty common among intermediate/advanced lifters. Its strength is in variety and recovery awareness, but each session being different can feel inconsistent if you prefer following the same program for several weeks — which is actually a real limitation for structured progression.

Here are the top alternatives worth considering:

**For true adaptive AI progression:**
- **FitnessAI** — built from millions of real training sessions, it adjusts in real time: if you're hitting sets easily it bumps weight, if you're struggling it pulls back. Very focused on intelligent progressive overload.
- **Alpha Progression** (~$60/yr) — bodybuilding-focused, uses RIR (reps in reserve) as a feedback loop, adapting sets and reps based on your logged performance. Great if hypertrophy is your main goal.

**For the most comprehensive AI system:**
- **Jefit** (~$40-70/yr) — runs 4 AI engines together: progressive overload, volume optimization, movement balance, and strength monitoring, all consolidated into a single progress score. More structured and data-rich than Fitbod.

**For recovery-integrated programming:**
- **Vora** — adjusts to your actual recovery state each day, integrating sleep, HRV, nutrition, and biometric data with your training. Best if you wear a fitness tracker and want truly adaptive loads.

**If budget isn't a concern:**
- **RP Strength** (~$300/yr) — trusted by Olympians and pro athletes, includes built-in periodization, automatic adjustments, and integrated nutrition. Arguably the most science-backed option.

**My honest take:** If your main gripe is that Fitbod's progression feels random rather than building toward something, **Alpha Progression or FitnessAI** are the most direct upgrades — they're built around structured, feedback-driven overload rather than workout variety. If you want the full picture including recovery data, **Vora** is worth a look. I'd try Alpha Progression first given your hypertrophy focus — it's cheap enough to test risk-free.

### CLAUDE (2026-04-10T19:42)
 You're not wrong — they're more similar than the marketing suggests. Both generate sessions, track sets/reps, and adjust based on logged performance. The RIR feedback is a bit more explicit in Alpha Progression, but the day-to-day feel is pretty much the same.

If Fitbod feels like preprogrammed workouts with a thin layer of adaptation, Alpha Progression likely won't fix that.

Given what you're describing — wanting something that **actually learns and responds** to you — a few better fits:

**Vora or Slate** if you want the app to factor in *how you're actually feeling/recovering*, not just what you lifted. That's a fundamentally different approach.

**RP Strength** if you're serious about gains and want periodization that builds in blocks with deloads — it's closer to how a real coach would program you, not just session-by-session tweaks.

**Caliber** if you want an actual human coach reviewing your data and adjusting your program. More expensive but a real step up in personalization.

The deeper issue might be that most "AI" lifting apps are still fundamentally reactive — they adjust *after* you log. A human coach or something like RP Strength's periodized structure is proactive, building toward a peak rather than just responding to last session.
