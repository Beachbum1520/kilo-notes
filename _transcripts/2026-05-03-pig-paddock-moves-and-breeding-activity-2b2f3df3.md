**Why the old way works better in your situation:**

Constant boar exposure is a known fertility booster — the boar's pheromones (androstenone in saliva) actually trigger and synchronize estrus in sows, especially in younger gilts. When you isolate sows and only introduce the boar at suspected heat, you lose that priming effect. You're also relying entirely on your eyes to catch a 24–48 hour standing window, which is brutal. Running together, Chester catches heats you'd miss, and the sows cycle more reliably because they're around him daily.

Your AI failures (Hazel Nov 7, Nutmeg Feb 7) may partly be a heat-detection problem rather than an AI problem — if you're not 100% sure she's in standing heat at the right hour, AI timing is off and conception drops hard.

**The hybrid model I'd suggest:**

Run the breeding herd together with Chester as the default. Use AI only when you specifically want a different sire's genetics (EL Macho, No Limit, etc.) on a particular sow — and for those, pull her out for the 2–3 day breeding window, AI her twice 12 hours apart, then return her to the group after a few days (long enough that Chester's mount won't out-compete the AI semen, typically 5–7 days post-AI to be safe).

This gives you:
- Higher overall settle rate from constant boar exposure
- Cleaner heat detection (you'll see the mounts)
- Targeted genetic improvement via AI on chosen sows
- Less labor watching for silent heats

**The tradeoff to plan for:**

Sire ambiguity. If Willow was AI'd Apr 2 and Chester is in the paddock with her now, and she didn't settle from the AI, Chester will catch her next cycle (~Apr 23, already past). **On separating for farrowing:**

Your instinct to pull them at 1–2 weeks pre-farrow is solid. Sows visibly bag up, get restless, start nesting. That's plenty of warning to get them into a clean farrowing pen without the stress of guessing exact dates.
