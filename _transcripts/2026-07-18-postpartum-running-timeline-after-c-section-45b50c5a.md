I'll keep my answer direct and concise without unnecessary coaching language, drawing on well-established return-to-running guidelines rather than needing to search for current information.Longer than most people expect. The standard framework (Goom/Donnelly/Brockwell return-to-running guidelines, widely adopted by pelvic health PTs):

- **0–6 weeks:** Walking only. C-section is major abdominal surgery — incision, fascia, and uterine healing take priority.
- **6–8 weeks:** OB clearance for general exercise. This is clearance to *begin* loading, not to run. Low-impact strength, core rehab, pelvic floor work.
- **12 weeks minimum** before any running, and only after passing a pelvic floor PT assessment — C-section doesn't spare the pelvic floor; pregnancy itself loads it for 9 months.
- **12 weeks–6 months:** Walk-run progression if she passes load tests (single-leg hop, jog in place, no leaking/heaviness/pain).

Practical timeline for Danielle: baby due July 2026 → PT evaluation around September/October → walk-run intervals late fall → continuous easy running realistically early 2027. That lines up exactly with the plan already in place: her return-to-running in early 2027, guide-runner block later that year, NYC November 2027. Roughly 10 months from continuous running to marathon start is workable for a guide-runner pace goal, especially with you handling pacing.

The one thing worth pushing: get the pelvic floor PT lined up in advance rather than waiting for symptoms. Most OBs won't proactively refer — she should ask for it at the 6-week visit.
