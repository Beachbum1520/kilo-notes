## What's happening during the "off" period

When you turn the pump off, several things happen over a minute or two:

**1. The suction line equalizes to static pressure.**

The pump stops pulling vacuum. Water in the line is now at the static pressure of the pool's water column above it (positive pressure, maybe 3-5 psi depending on how high the line sits relative to pool water level).

**2. Any air pocket that had accumulated gets pushed out.**

Air that was sucked in during operation and trapped in the line (in high points, at the pump basket, in the filter) gets displaced as water flows in to refill. Some air burps out the pump lid area, some goes up to the filter and out through the return.

**3. Water flows OUT through the crack into the soil.**

This is the key part. The crack now has water on the inside (under positive pressure) and soil on the outside. Water seeps out through the crack into the surrounding soil. This is the same mechanism causing your "faster than evaporation" water loss last summer.

**4. The soil around the crack becomes saturated.**

Wet, muddy soil with no air gaps. The void spaces in the soil that previously held air are now filled with water leaking from the line.

## What's happening when you turn the pump back on

**Phase 1 (first few minutes): Pump works fine.**

The line is full of water, no air pocket. Pump primes immediately and pulls solid water. The crack is now under vacuum, but it's surrounded by **saturated soil with no air available**. Water gets pulled back IN through the crack instead of air — because that's what's available.

Result: pump runs clean, no air at the cell.

**Phase 2 (minutes to hours): Soil dries out around the crack.**

As the pump pulls water in through the crack, the saturated soil around the crack starts to dewater. Water from the crack zone gets sucked into the line and replaced by... well, eventually, by air seeping in from drier soil further away from the crack.

The crack zone transitions from "saturated mud" to "moist soil" to "soil with air pockets."

Result: air starts to be ingested. Bubbling at the cell begins, gradually.

**Phase 3 (extended operation): Air dominates the leak path.**

The soil around the crack is now relatively dry. Air pockets in the surrounding dirt are the dominant feature near the crack. The vacuum pulls air through these voids and into the line.

Result: progressive worsening. More and more air, less water flow.

**Phase 4 (eventually): Pump prime is compromised.**

If enough air accumulates faster than the pump can move it through to the discharge, the pump basket develops an air pocket, prime is lost, and water flow drops dramatically.

## Why turning the pump off "resets" the system

Off period → positive pressure → water leaks OUT into soil → soil re-saturates around the crack → next startup sees saturated soil → no air available initially → pump works clean for a while → cycle repeats.

This is **exactly** your observed behavior. The "few minutes off" lets the soil re-saturate. The longer the off period, the longer the clean operation when you restart.

## Why this pattern doesn't fit most other failure modes

**Pump lid O-ring**: Would leak air consistently from the start, not improve after restart. The seal is either good or bad — it doesn't "reset" with an off period.

**Pump drain plug O-ring**: Same as above. Consistent, not progressive.

**Threaded adapter at pump**: Same. The threads don't change their seal based on pump cycle.

**Pad-side fitting (flex PVC glue joint)**: Would also be consistent, not progressive. A glue joint is either holding or it isn't.

**Skimmer (if it were a candidate, which we've ruled out)**: Doesn't have a mechanism for "resetting" between cycles.

**Buried suction line crack in soil**: ONLY this failure mode produces the off/on cycling behavior you describe, because the soil's water content around the crack changes with the pump cycle.

This isn't just "consistent with" the buried elbow theory. It's a **signature** of underground suction-line leaks. Pool service technicians use this pattern as a diagnostic indicator specifically.

Roughly:

- **Off-to-saturated**: A few minutes is enough for water to seep back into immediately surrounding soil. Several hours of off-time saturates a wider zone.
- **Clean operation after restart**: Depends on how much soil got resaturated. Few minutes off → maybe 10-30 minutes of clean running. Overnight off → could be hours of clean running.
- **Onset of air ingestion**: Gradual, starts as occasional bubbles
- **Progressive worsening**: Accelerates as soil dries out
- **Loss of prime / barely moving water**: Eventually, if you don't intervene
