**It helps the slab cure.** Formed edges hold moisture in the concrete instead of letting the sides flash-dry in the sun and wind. Concrete cures by staying damp, not by drying out, so forms on = a little better side cure. Especially with the July heat, that's working in your favor.

The couple of things to be aware of, none of them dealbreakers:

**Treated lumber left long against concrete can stain the edge slightly** and can get harder to strip as it swells and grips. Not structural, just cosmetic and a little more prying. On a livestock pad nobody cares about a faint edge stain.

**Screws can get concrete crust in the heads.** You bedded these to reuse — after a week the screw heads may have a little cured slop in them. Wire-brush the heads before you back them out so you don't strip a driver bit. Minor.

**If you want to reuse these exact forms on the next pour, a week's fine but don't leave them all summer.** The longer treated lumber sits wet against concrete, the more it cups and the crustier it gets. A week or two, no issue. A month-plus and you're fighting warped, crusted boards.

So: leave them on as long as it's convenient this week — the edges and cure both benefit. When you do strip, wire-brush the screw heads, back the screws out, and gently pry rather than hammer so you don't chip that nice edge. Then clean the crust off the boards before they dry hard if you're rolling them onto the next job.

Only real reason to pull them sooner would be if you need the boards for the next pour — but you've got the chute and well pads coming on a ready-mix truck, so these waterer forms aren't in the critical path anyway. Leave 'em be.
