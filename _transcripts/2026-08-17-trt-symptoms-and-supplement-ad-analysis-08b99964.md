**No. This is a supplement ad wearing a doctor's letter as a costume.** The physiology is real enough to feel credible; the conclusion — buy Aurivita Capsaicin Power — is where it falls apart.

**The tells, before anything medical:**
- The account is "Dr. Mike Harris," the letter is signed "Dr. Daniel Westbrook," and the photo is stock. Marked "Ad."
- It says 19 years of practice in paragraph one and 15 years two paragraphs later.
- Aurivita is a Shopify/Amazon brand (Azeal Supplements LLC), shipped from China, with the same cayenne-D3-K2-beetroot formula sold under a dozen white-label names. This is the standard long-form advertorial funnel: 4,000 words of medical narrative, one product, no exit.

**What's actually true in it:**
- ED often precedes a cardiac event by a few years — that's the real Montorsi work in *European Urology*. ED is a legitimate vascular warning sign.
- TRT does raise hematocrit; hematocrit in the mid-50s does raise thrombotic risk and warrants attention.
- Tamsulosin does cause retrograde ejaculation by relaxing the bladder neck.
- David Julius did win the 2021 Nobel for TRPV1 — for discovering how we sense heat and pain. It has nothing to do with a softgel.

**What's false:**
- The central claim that cypionate is driving "systemic vascular destruction" toward a cardiac event. TRAVERSE — 5,204 hypogonadal men with existing cardiovascular disease or high risk, randomized against placebo — found testosterone noninferior to placebo for cardiac death, MI, and stroke. It did flag more arrhythmias, VTE, and fractures, which is why monitoring matters — but that's a monitoring story, not a countdown clock.
- Reduced ejaculate volume on TRT is mostly endocrine, not vascular: suppressed LH/FSH means less seminal and prostatic fluid. The ad quietly swaps that for a vascular explanation because vascular is what its product claims to fix.
- "3mg in cold-pressed avocado oil takes absorption from 10% to 90%" is invented precision. No clinical trial establishes a capsaicin dose for BPH, ED, or endothelial repair.
- "Three to five years before it reaches your heart" is a fear timer, not a finding.

**The part worth taking seriously:** your symptoms. Not because the ad says so — because low ejaculate volume, fading erections, and urinary changes on multi-year TRT are exactly the cluster that deserves real numbers rather than a supplement. Worth pulling: hematocrit/hemoglobin, PSA, estradiol, blood pressure, a lipid panel, and — given your age and years on therapy — a coronary calcium score, which is cheap and settles the actual question this ad is trying to scare you about. If your hematocrit is genuinely running 54+, that's a dose or protocol conversation with whoever manages your TRT, not a cayenne capsule.

### CLAUDE (2026-08-17T12:17)

Highest-yield lever first: **hCG added to your existing protocol.** That's the standard answer for exactly this complaint, and it's the one thing that addresses the actual mechanism.

**Why volume dropped.** Exogenous testosterone shuts down LH/FSH, which collapses intratesticular testosterone and testicular/epididymal output. Your serum T looks great; the local signaling downstream doesn't. Your erections being fine and PSA being fine is consistent with this — it's an endocrine suppression problem, not the vascular story that ad was selling.

**What to raise with whoever manages your TRT:**

- **hCG, 500–1500 IU 2–3x/week alongside your current dose.** Evidence for volume specifically is case-series level, not RCT — the strong data is on restoring spermatogenesis. But mechanistically it's the right target, and restored volume is a commonly reported effect. Expect 8–12 weeks before judging it.
- **Check estradiol.** If you're on anastrozole, over-suppressed E2 is a well-known cause of low ejaculate volume and reduced sensation. Crushing E2 to "look good on paper" is a frequent own-goal.
- **Post-ejaculate urinalysis** — cheap, one visit, and rules out partial retrograde flow. If you're on any alpha-blocker, finasteride/dutasteride, or an SSRI, that's a separate and often bigger contributor than the TRT.
- **Force is a separate muscle problem.** Expulsion is bulbospongiosus/pelvic floor. Pelvic floor training has real evidence for ejaculatory force and post-void dribble, and it's free. Volume and force can be fixed independently.

**The tradeoff worth naming up front:** hCG stimulates your testes, which nudges hematocrit and estradiol *up*. You're already running high hematocrit. So going in, the plan should be hCG plus something on the other side of the ledger — splitting your cypionate into smaller twice-weekly or EOD doses lowers peak-driven erythropoiesis, and therapeutic phlebotomy or regular blood donation manages the number directly. Don't add hCG without a hematocrit recheck at 8 weeks.

Simple things that also move volume: 2–3 days of abstinence before, and actual hydration.

Worth finding a urologist or men's health doc who manages hCG routinely rather than a clinic that only knows how to titrate cypionate.

### SCOTT (2026-08-17T12:21)
What is hcg?

### CLAUDE (2026-08-17T12:21)
Human chorionic gonadotropin — a hormone, given by injection.

Structurally it mimics LH, the pituitary signal that tells your testes to make testosterone. That's why it matters for you: when you inject cypionate, your pituitary stops sending LH, your testes go quiet, and intratesticular testosterone drops to a fraction of normal even though your bloodwork looks perfect. hCG substitutes for the missing signal, so the testes keep working alongside the TRT rather than idling.

It's a prescription drug, sold as Pregnyl or Novarel in the US, typically injected subcutaneously 2–3x/week with an insulin syringe — same routine you already have. It's most established for preserving fertility and testicular volume on TRT; the ejaculate-volume benefit rides on the same mechanism.

Practical note: not every TRT clinic offers it, and pharmacy access got more restrictive after FDA reclassified it as a biologic in 2020, which limited compounded versions. Brand-name is still prescribable — just ask specifically.

### CLAUDE (2026-08-17T12:22)
Pregnyl — that's the spelling. Merck/Organon brand, hCG derived from urine, sold as a powder vial you reconstitute with the supplied diluent.

Novarel is the same thing from Ferring. Ovidrel is a different form — recombinant hCG in a prefilled syringe, more common in fertility clinics and usually pricier for ongoing use.

For a standing TRT protocol, Pregnyl or Novarel is the usual pick. One 10,000 IU vial reconstituted lasts a while at 500–1500 IU per shot. Keep it refrigerated once mixed — reconstituted hCG has a limited shelf life, typically around 30–60 days depending on the diluent, so ask the pharmacist what yours is rated for.
