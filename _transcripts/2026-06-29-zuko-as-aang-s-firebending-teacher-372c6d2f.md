Aang masters all four and wins — but the *how* is the interesting part, and it's not a straight "punch everyone into oblivion" ending.

Quick spoiler flag since the Netflix Season 3 hasn't aired this arc yet, though this is how the original series resolves it:

Aang does master earth and fire to go with air and water, so he's got the full set heading into the finale. And the final showdown against Fire Lord Ozai during Sozin's Comet (when firebenders are at peak power) is a genuinely huge, world-tier fight — he absolutely holds his own and then some.

But the twist is that Aang, as an Air Nomad raised by monks, refuses to kill Ozai even though basically everyone tells him he has to. He wrestles hard with it, because the "obvious" path is to end the tyrant permanently. Instead he unlocks a fifth ability — **energybending** — and strips Ozai of his firebending entirely, leaving him powerless rather than dead. So he wins decisively without compromising his core principle of not taking a life.

Meanwhile the rest of the Gaang is doing the other heavy lifting in parallel — Zuko and Katara take on Azula in the famous Agni Kai duel, Sokka/Toph/Suki wreck the Fire Nation airship fleet, etc. It's an ensemble win, not a solo curbstomp.

So: yes, he learns them all and wins big — but the show deliberately makes the climax about *restraint and conviction* rather than just raw power, which is part of why the ending landed so well. Whether the Netflix version keeps the energybending resolution or tweaks it is the open question for Season 3.
