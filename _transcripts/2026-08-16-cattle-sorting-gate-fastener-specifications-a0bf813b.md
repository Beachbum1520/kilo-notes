### CLAUDE (2026-08-16T13:22)
**1/2" × 5" hot-dipped galvanized lag screws with HDG washers — two per rail end, staggered diagonally.** Pilot 5/16" into the post, 1/2" clearance hole through the 2x6.

A few things that matter more than the fastener size:

- **Two fasteners per board per post, always.** One lag lets the board pivot on the round post, and a pivoting board works the lag loose. Stagger them high/low, not on the same grain line, or you'll split the 2x6.
- **Rails on the inside face of the lane.** Cattle pressure then pushes the board into the post instead of pulling the lags out in withdrawal. Lags are strong in shear, weak in pull-out.
- **Flatten a landing.** Round post + flat board = tangent-line contact only. Chainsaw or adze a ~6" flat where each rail lands. Without it the board rocks, the lags loosen, and torquing them down splits the rail.
- **HDG or stainless only** — treated posts and rails will eat electroplated zinc.

On the gate post (the middle one): don't lag the hinges. Through-bolt it — 5/8" or 3/4" HDG machine bolts all the way through with washer and nut on the back side. A 6' gate cantilevered on a swing post will pull lags out over a season or two.

**Caveat on the size:** I'm judging post diameter from the photo, and they read as roughly 5–6". If any post is under about 5" through the face where the rail lands, drop to 3/8" × 4" so the lag tip doesn't come out the back. Worth measuring before you buy.

Alternative if you'd rather skip the predrill: FastenMaster TimberLOK 6" or GRK RSS 5" structural screws drive straight in. Faster, and shear strength is fine for this — but I'd still through-bolt the gate post.

### CLAUDE (2026-08-16T13:24)
Your posts are round; your 2x6 is flat. A flat board laid against a curved surface only touches along a single vertical line — the point where the board is tangent to the circle. Everywhere else there's a gap that widens as you move toward the board's edges.

Two problems come from that:

- **Rocking.** With contact on one narrow line, the board can pivot on it like a seesaw. Cattle lean on the rail, it rocks, and the lag works loose over time.
- **Splitting.** Your instinct will be to crank the lags down until the board sits tight. But you're pulling the outer part of a 5½"-wide board across a gap onto a curve. That bends it, and treated pine splits along the grain rather than conforming.

The fix is to make the post flat where the board lands. Take a chainsaw and cut a shallow, flat face — maybe 5½–6" tall, an inch or so deep at the center, just enough that a straightedge sits flush. One flat per rail per post, so four on each post if you're running four rails.

Two notes: cut only as deep as you need, since you're removing wood from the post's cross-section and cutting through the pressure-treated shell into less-protected heartwood. Brush the fresh cut with copper naphthenate end-cut solution before the board goes on.

The alternative is to skip the flats and just run the boards on, but shim behind each end. That's more fussy and the shims eventually work out. The chainsaw flat is the standard approach.

### CLAUDE (2026-08-16T14:53)
Worth a second look before you settle — Home Depot usually stocks HDG lags loose in the bulk fastener bins rather than in the boxed hardware aisle. Different section, easy to miss. Same for Tractor Supply if that's closer.

If it's truly not there, better options than plated zinc:

- **Structural screws** — GRK RSS or FastenMaster TimberLOK. Proprietary coatings rated for ACQ/MCA treated lumber, and HD carries both. 6" TimberLOK or 5" RSS drives without a pilot in the post. This is probably your easiest swap.
- **Order HDG online**, ship to store, couple of days.

The reason it matters: modern treated lumber uses copper-based preservatives that eat electroplated zinc. The plating is maybe a tenth the thickness of hot-dip. In a wet post with treated rails, plated lags can start rusting through in a few years rather than a few decades.

That said — you've already made the call that this is a maintain-and-replace build, and plated lags fit that. If you go that route, just know you're likely looking at replacement on a 3–5 year horizon instead of 20+, and rust streaks down the posts as the tell.

### CLAUDE (2026-08-16T14:58)
4" would work — 2½" of thread into the post is past the usual minimum for a rail, and it's easier driving.

But I'd stay with the 5" you're holding. Reasons:

Got it off the Kubota diagram. The grip is held on by a **tapping screw, part K7591-40040** — not a machine screw or a socket cap screw. That's consistent with the hex recess you're seeing; it's a hex-head self-tapper driven with a nut driver, not an Allen key.

For the substitute: since it's a self-tapper into the grip boss, the fit is set by the existing hole, not a thread standard. Measure the hole diameter and depth, then match a hex-head tapping screw to it at the hardware store. Slightly undersized is recoverable; oversized will split the plastic boss.

### CLAUDE (2026-08-27T16:11)
Yes, that'll work fine for this. A couple of practical notes:

- **Check for HDG fender washers first.** Home Depot usually stocks them in the same bulk bins — 3/8" ID, galvanized. Saves you the step.
- **If you do spray:** hit both faces, and let them cure fully before installing. Cold galvanizing compound is zinc-rich paint, so it's real zinc protection, but it's a film rather than a bonded coating.
- **Know what won't survive:** the face under the bolt head will scrape off as you torque it down. That's unavoidable and not really a problem — the face against the wood is the one that matters, and that one stays intact.

No galvanic issue mixing sprayed-zinc washers with HDG lags. Both zinc, same potential.

One genuine upside: fender washers have more bearing area than standard flats, which is better on a soft treated 2x6. Less chance of the head crushing into the board when you snug it. If they're sitting right there, I'd take them over standard washers even setting the coating question aside.
