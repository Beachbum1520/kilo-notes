### CLAUDE (2026-09-04T16:49)
This looks like a mixed succulent arrangement — three different plants sharing one pot, not a single species:

- **Jade plant** (*Crassula ovata*) — the classic thick oval green leaves on branching stems, front and center.
- **Finger/stick succulent** — the narrow tubular pale-green leaves fanning out on the left, likely a *Crassula* variety (possibly "Ogre Ears" or a similar finger jade) or could be a *Senecio* — hard to be 100% certain without a closer look at the leaf tips.
- **Paddle-leaf succulent** — the large blue-gray/lavender flat leaves, most likely *Kalanchoe thyrsiflora* ("Flapjack" plant) or a *Pachyphytum*/*Graptopetalum* type.

**General care for this combo** (they mostly share the same needs, which is why this works as a planter):

- **Light**: Bright, indirect to full sun. The purplish tint on the paddle leaves actually intensifies with more sun exposure — that's a good sign, not stress.
- **Water**: Let soil dry out completely between waterings. Water deeply, then ignore it for 1–2 weeks. This is the #1 killer of succulents — overwatering, not underwatering.
- **Soil/drainage**: Needs to drain fast — a cactus/succulent mix, and the pot must have a drainage hole (your outer white pot may not, so make sure it's just a cachepot around a draining inner pot, which it looks like it is).

**One thing to address now**: I can see a fair amount of brown/dried, shriveled leaf matter tucked in at the base between the plants. That's worth pulling out — dead leaf litter sitting against the stems in a pot that's already been watered heavily (things look quite wet in these photos) is a good setup for rot or fungus gnats. I'd let this pot dry out fully before the next watering and clean out that dead material while it's accessible.

### CLAUDE (2026-09-04T16:50)
Correct — these aren't like orchids, which are epiphytes evolved for filtered light under a rainforest canopy and will scorch in direct sun. Jade, kalanchoe, and finger-leaf succulents evolved in exposed, arid, high-UV environments (South Africa, Madagascar) and are built for it.

One caveat, not a contradiction: sudden transition matters more than sun intensity itself. If this plant has been sitting indoors or in shade and you move it straight into full outdoor midday sun, the leaves can sunburn (bleached or brown patches) simply because they haven't built up UV tolerance yet — same reason a pale-skinned person burns on day one of a beach trip. Acclimate over 1-2 weeks: a few hours of morning sun, increasing daily, before leaving it in full sun all day.

Once acclimated, full sun is where the color actually improves — that purple tint on the paddle leaves gets deeper and richer with more light, not less.
