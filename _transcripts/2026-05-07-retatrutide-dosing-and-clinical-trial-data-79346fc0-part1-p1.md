- Weeks 5–8: 4 mg weekly
- Weeks 9–12: 8 mg (or held at 4)

### CLAUDE (2026-05-07T13:31)
 Good question — this matters more than most people realize, and the difference is genuinely meaningful for your situation.

**The two ways labs measure estradiol**

**Immunoassay (what Quest ran on you — value of 40)**

Uses antibodies that bind to estradiol. Cheap, fast, automated, runs on the same machines doing thousands of other hormone tests. This is what most standard panels use by default.

The problem: the antibodies aren't perfectly specific to estradiol. They cross-react with other steroid hormones, estrogen metabolites, and structurally similar compounds. The assay was originally optimized for *women* — premenopausal female E2 ranges from 30-400+ pg/mL, so the noise floor of cross-reactivity (5-15 pg/mL of "false signal") doesn't matter much.

In **men**, where true E2 typically runs 10-40 pg/mL, that same noise floor of cross-reactivity becomes a huge percentage of the reading. If your true E2 is 25 and the assay adds 10-15 from cross-reactivity, you read as 35-40 — which is exactly what your Quest result showed.

The Quest report literally tells you this in the footnote: *"For any patients for whom low Estradiol levels are anticipated (e.g. males, pre-pubertal children and hypogonadal/post-menopausal females), the Quest Diagnostics Nichols Institute Estradiol, Ultrasensitive, LCMSMS assay is recommended."*

They're telling you the result on your panel is unreliable for a man.

**LC/MS/MS (Liquid Chromatography / Tandem Mass Spectrometry)**

This is the reference-standard method. Two-step process:

1. **Liquid chromatography** physically separates estradiol from other compounds in your blood based on molecular properties. The estradiol gets isolated from everything else.

2. **Tandem mass spectrometry** then identifies and quantifies *only* estradiol by measuring its exact molecular mass and a fragment ion specific to estradiol's structure.

Result: no cross-reactivity, no antibody noise, measures only the actual estradiol molecule. Accurate down to 1-2 pg/mL.

It's slower (batch processing), more expensive ($60-100 vs $20-30 for immunoassay), and requires specialized equipment, which is why it's not the default.
