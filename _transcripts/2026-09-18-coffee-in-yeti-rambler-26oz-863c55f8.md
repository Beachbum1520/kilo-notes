### CLAUDE (2026-09-18T18:01)
The 26oz Rambler holds about 3¼ standard 8oz cups, or a bit over two 12oz mugs, of coffee.

Practical notes:
- **Fit for pour-over/drip:** it's a straight-sided bottle-style tumbler (not the wide-mouth tumbler), so it works fine as a receiving vessel but you can't fit a filter cone on top of it — brew separately and pour in.
- **Cap options:** if you get it with the Chug Cap, it's not spill-proof for sipping while driving; the Straw Cap or Hotshot Cap (screw-on, more sip-friendly) work better for coffee on the move.
- **Heat retention:** double-wall vacuum insulation keeps it drinkably hot for roughly 6+ hours, scalding-hot for less than that — Yeti's marketed "hot for 24 hours" number is really just "warmer than room temp," not usable.
- **Cleaning:** stainless steel, dishwasher safe, but coffee/tannins will stain the interior over time — a baking soda or denture-tablet soak clears it.

If you're deciding between the 26oz Bottle vs. the 20oz or 30oz Tumbler for coffee specifically, the tumbler styles are easier to drink from directly (wider mouth) — the bottle is better if you want a sealed cap for a bag or truck console.

### CLAUDE (2026-09-18T18:02)
Yeah, the 26oz Rambler Bottle comes standard with the Chug Cap.

With that cap:
- **No slow-sip capability** — it's a wide open spout, so it pours like tipping back a bottle. Fine at a desk or in a cupholder when stationary, not great for sipping in the truck without risking a faceful of hot coffee at a bump.
- **Fully removable, no tether** — easy to lose in a truck console or on a jobsite.
- **Easiest of the caps to clean** — no straw or small parts, just rinse.
- **Not leakproof, but spill-resistant** when upright — it'll survive being knocked over briefly, not submersion or being on its side in a bag.

If you want something more drink-and-drive friendly, Yeti sells the **Straw Cap** and **Hotshot Cap** separately — both are compatible with the same 26oz bottle, so you don't need a new bottle, just swap the cap.

### CLAUDE (2026-09-18T18:03)
For that use, the 26oz Rambler with the Chug Cap works well as a coffee carrier — a few things worth knowing:

- **Heat hold on the drive:** with the farm not far off, coffee should still be solidly hot on arrival — vacuum insulation keeps it near-scalding for a few hours, so a drive of well under an hour is no problem at all.
- **Chug Cap in a moving truck:** the open spout means splashing if you try to sip while driving over rough farm roads. Fine if you're just transporting it and drinking once parked, less fine if you want to sip at stoplights on the way.
- **Capacity:** 26oz covers a "2nd cup" easily — that's more like 2+ standard mugs, so you've got headroom if you want to fill it fuller.
- **One gotcha:** the Chug Cap isn't fully sealed, so if it tips over in the cupholder or on a truck seat, you'll get some leakage — worth keeping it upright in a cupholder rather than on the seat itself, especially heading down to Watts Way on gravel/dirt.

If you find yourself wanting to actually sip while driving out to the farm, the Straw Cap swaps onto the same bottle and solves that — otherwise the Chug Cap is genuinely the simpler, easier-to-clean choice for a straightforward "fill it, drive it, drink it parked" routine.
