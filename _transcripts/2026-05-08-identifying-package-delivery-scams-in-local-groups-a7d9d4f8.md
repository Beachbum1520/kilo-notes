### CLAUDE (2026-05-08T12:22)
 Good instinct to be suspicious. This is almost certainly a scam, and there are a few well-documented variants going around in local Facebook groups right now. Here's the breakdown:

**How they get the pics**

The photos are lifted from FedEx/UPS/Amazon delivery confirmation emails or app notifications. A few ways that happens:
- Someone's email gets compromised and the scammer scrapes delivery photos
- Photos shared publicly in "where's my package" posts get reused
- Some are pulled from prior scam posts in other towns (same image, different "Melissa" in a different group)
- Occasionally a household member's phone/email is the leak point

The giveaway: the photo is generic enough (just a garage and a box) that it could plausibly be anywhere, which is exactly why they pick those.

**What the scam actually is**

A few flavors, in rough order of likelihood for this kind of post:

1. **Account farming / engagement bait.** "Rising contributor" badge is the tell. They're building up a profile in a local group with sympathetic posts (lost package, lost dog, missing kid) to establish trust. Once the account looks legitimate and local, they either sell it, or pivot it to push rental scams, fake fundraisers, marketplace fraud, or phishing links. The 6-year-old daughter detail is emotional manipulation to maximize shares — shares = reach = value.

2. **Comment harvesting / DM pivot.** When sympathetic neighbors comment "that looks like the house on X street" or "I think I know who lives there," the scammer now has reconnaissance for a future scam (or sells the info). They may also DM commenters with a follow-up hook.

3. **Lead-in to a payment ask.** Later post: "Found out the box had my daughter's birthday gift, I can't afford to replace it, Cashapp $..."  You won't see this until they've built enough goodwill.

4. **Phishing via "View more details."** Less likely in a native FB post but worth noting — sometimes these link out.

**Why you can be confident it's not real**

- "Rising contributor" + 2-hour-old post + emotional kid hook is the standard scam signature
- Hogansville is ~30 min from Franklin — close enough to feel local, far enough that nobody can easily verify
- The photo predates the post by an unknown amount; you got a real package, she's claiming it's hers weeks later
- Real lost-package situations go through the carrier, not Facebook crowdsourcing

**What to do**

Report the post to the group admins as a scam, don't engage in comments, and don't DM her. If you want to be thorough, reverse-image-search the garage photo (Google Lens) — high odds it shows up in other towns' Facebook groups with a different "Melissa."

Your package is yours. Carpet cleaner part stays with the carpet cleaner.

So: she has a real photo of *your* garage with *your* package on the ground, and she's claiming the package is hers. That changes the analysis significantly.

**How she got the photo**

Almost certainly the FedEx delivery confirmation photo. Two realistic paths:

1. **Wrong tracking number / misrouted notification.** FedEx occasionally sends delivery confirmation emails to the wrong customer when tracking numbers get crossed, addresses are similar, or there's a data entry error. She may have been expecting a package, gotten *your* delivery photo in her FedEx app or email, and concluded the driver dropped it at the wrong house.
2. **Her package was actually misdelivered somewhere else** and she's pattern-matching — saw a delivery photo that wasn't her house, posted it hoping the homeowner would recognize it.

The "Hogansville Chatty" group is a regional group, and Franklin is close enough that her post could plausibly reach you.

**Why this is probably not malicious**

- She's not asking for money
- She's not asking you to click anything
- She acknowledges upfront she probably won't get it back
- The "Rising contributor" badge in a small-town group is normal for an active local poster, not necessarily a red flag
- The emotional framing (6-year-old daughter) reads as genuine frustration, not a setup

This looks like a real person who genuinely believes her package was delivered to your house — because FedEx's system told her so, with a photo of your garage as "proof."

**What's actually going on**

Two packages, two tracking numbers, one of them got crossed in FedEx's system. Either:
- Her carpet cleaner part is sitting at someone else's house (and they're confused too), or
- The photo attached to her tracking number is wrong, but her package did arrive somewhere — possibly even her own porch
- Or her package is genuinely lost and FedEx attached your delivery photo to her tracking by mistake

**What to do**

You don't owe her anything and you have zero obligation to engage. But if you want to be neighborly: a quick comment or DM saying "That's my garage, but the package was a part I ordered for my own equipment — sounds like FedEx crossed your tracking number with mine, you should call them" would resolve her confusion and point her at the right fix (FedEx claim).

The scam instinct was wrong here, but it wasn't a bad instinct — these posts *do* get used as scam vectors, and skepticism is the right default. This one just happens to be the boring explanation.
