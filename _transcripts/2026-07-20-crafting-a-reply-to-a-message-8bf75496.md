### CLAUDE (2026-07-20T18:12)
A few things I'd separate before drafting, because Par is blending two different questions:

**1. The mechanics are already settled.** Ayesha's position is contractually correct and mostly moot as a "penalty" debate. Take today's chain (7/20) as written notice — the clock runs to ~10/18, hotel closes 8/11, so the actual delta in dispute is roughly two months of MRC billed post-closure. That's the number this decision is about. Nobody on the thread has quantified it, which means everyone's arguing philosophy instead of dollars. Get the MRC first.

**2. Par's goodwill argument is weaker than it sounds.** The paying entity is dissolving — the hotel is sold and converting to apartments. There's no client to retain and the final invoice goes to an ownership group exiting the business, not to Marriott corporate. The person whose perception arguably matters is Tony Ellis, who'll be a GM somewhere else in the Marriott system — that's real but modest. Also note Par just put BPRF's Schedule 6 execution miss in writing as the justification for a concession. That's a self-inflicted admission on a thread with a wide cc; you don't want that framing repeated, especially with GRE&T live.

**3. This isn't the thread's decision anyway.** Waiving ~2 months of revenue is a P&L concession — that's Jady's call, not something to settle by consensus among Par, accounting, and Kathy. Your move is to close the debate, set the default (contract governs), and route the exception path cleanly. That also keeps Kathy informed without giving her a vote.

**4. Two things to verify before anyone commits:** (a) what the property agreement actually says about sale/closure — many have assignment or early-termination provisions for change of ownership that could override the 90-day math entirely; (b) Tony said Marriott's Transition Team is issuing a mass vendor cancellation — that formal notice may reset the notice date or the billing contact. Don't finalize the invoice off a GM's email if a corporate notice is coming.

One side note: the building is getting internet as an apartment dwelling. That's not a BPRF play, but it's a clean Cox Business referral — small gesture, costs nothing, and it's the kind of thing that reads well internally in the merger context.
