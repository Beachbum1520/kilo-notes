### CLAUDE (2026-08-14T11:47)

**The reason she gave you isn't real.** No Philippine bank requires marriage or a child before a Filipino citizen can receive money from a foreigner. That rule does not exist. The Philippines takes in roughly $38B/year in remittances, a large share of it between people who aren't married or related.

What's actually required: BPI opens a deposit account with one valid ID, in about five minutes. Basic deposit accounts are BSP-mandated, low-requirement, and need ₱0–100 to open. The PhilSys National ID is accepted at BDO, BPI, UnionBank, Metrobank, Landbank, Security Bank, and RCBC — banks are required to accept it.

Practical options, easiest first:
- **GCash or Maya** — e-wallet, one ID, self-onboard on the phone in minutes. Receives international remittances directly.
- **GoTyme** — kiosk at any Robinsons or Puregold, card printed on the spot.
- **BPI/BDO branch** — one valid ID, small opening deposit.

Two things that don't add up, and I'd say this plainly whether or not you want to hear it:

She has a job. In the Philippines almost all employers pay through payroll ATM accounts, so she very likely already has one. If she does, there's no reason to open a second account to receive money from you.

The specific story — went to the bank, got emotionally invested, came home empty-handed with selfies — is the standard opening move in a well-worn script. The next step is usually one of: a fee to "unblock" or "activate" the account, a tax on incoming remittance (doesn't exist), a request to send to a third party's account "temporarily," or pressure toward marriage/fiancé paperwork.

A clean test, no confrontation needed: tell her to install GCash and verify it herself tonight, and that you'll send there. It takes ten minutes and costs nothing. If that becomes complicated too, you have your answer.

Firm rules regardless: never send to an account not in her legal name, never pay a fee to enable a transfer, and don't send anything until you've seen her face and her ID on a live video call you initiated.

A new-accounts officer hearing "my American boyfriend sends me an allowance" may well have discouraged her and improvised the marriage/child part as shorthand for "a documented relationship." **Change branches or banks.** Branch-level discretion in PH is enormous. Same bank, different branch, different outcome. BPI and UnionBank are generally the least friction.

**Skip the teller entirely.** GoTyme at a mall kiosk, or app-based onboarding with BPI/UnionBank/Maya — no conversation, no oversharing, one valid ID. If she has the PhilSys National ID, banks are required to accept it as a primary ID.

**Or just use her existing payroll account.** If her employer pays via ATM account, remittances land there fine. A second account may be solving a problem she doesn't have.

On the sending side, Wise or Remitly to her bank account is cheapest. GCash works too via Ria and similar partners. One constraint worth knowing: a fully verified GCash wallet caps at ₱100,000 balance, and inbound remittances from all providers count against monthly limits together — fine for a recurring allowance, awkward for a large one-time transfer. For anything sizable, bank account beats wallet.

### CLAUDE (2026-08-14T11:53)
Understood — that settles it, and I won't raise it again.

Now the actual answer: **bank-to-bank is the most expensive option, not the cheapest.** Your instinct is backwards here. Let me check current numbers.

**Switch to Wise. Don't do a bank wire.**

Why the bank idea fails: there's no ACH between the US and PH — "bank to bank" means a SWIFT wire. That's $35–50 outgoing from your US bank, $10–25 taken by BPI/BDO on the receiving end, and a 2.5–4% FX markup buried in the rate. On $1,000 you'd lose $70–90. It's the worst channel available, and it only looks cheap because the markup is invisible.

The bigger point: **your Remitly cost probably isn't mostly the fee you're seeing.** Remitly advertises low or zero fees but bakes the margin into the exchange rate, so the recipient just gets fewer pesos. That markup runs 0.5%–3.0% over mid-market. On a recurring allowance, the spread dwarfs the visible fee.

Wise charges the real mid-market rate with the fee shown separately. Current USD→PHP cost is roughly $3.50 fixed plus a small percentage — call it $8–10 on $1,000 all-in, versus $25–40 on Remitly's regular rate. On $2,000/month, the difference between the best and worst channel runs ₱3,000–6,000 monthly.

For the auto-draft you wanted: Wise supports scheduled recurring transfers funded by ACH pull from your US bank, paying out to her BPI/BDO account or GCash. That's exactly the "set it and forget it" structure you're after, just on better rails.

Two things to check before you commit:
- **How you're funding Remitly now.** Debit/credit card funding carries a ~3% surcharge on top of everything else. If you're doing that, some of your pain is self-inflicted and switching to ACH funding helps regardless of provider.
- **Run one live quote side by side.** Same amount, same day, and compare pesos *delivered*, not fees charged. That's the only comparison that matters, and it'll show you the real gap in about two minutes.

One structural lever: if you're sending weekly, consolidate to monthly. The fixed component gets amortized over 4x the amount.
