Two problems with that plan, one gating and one conceptual.

**The gating one is material participation.** Farm losses only offset your wage income if you materially participate under §469 — realistically the 500-hour test, or the 100-hour test if nobody else puts in more. You're running a managed WiFi business with a 100+ person offshore team and traveling to the Philippines. If the IRS ever asks, "did you spend 500 hours on Watts Way Farms in 2026," you need a contemporaneous log, not a reconstruction. Without material participation the losses are passive, suspended, and do nothing for you this year. This is the single most likely thing to break the strategy, and it's worth confirming before you build around it.

Behind that sits §183. A direct-to-consumer beef and pork operation needs profit in three of five years to hold the presumption. A large loss claimed in the exact year your W-2 income doubles is a recognizable pattern.

**The conceptual one is that you're targeting the wrong thing.** Brackets apply to taxable income, not MAGI, and they're marginal — dropping below the 35% threshold only changes the rate on the dollars that crossed it. Every legitimate farm loss dollar is already saving you about 35 cents whether or not you clear a bracket line. There's no cliff to chase.

Run the magnitude: at roughly $700K gross, you'd need something in the neighborhood of $170K of deductible farm loss to get taxable income under the 35% threshold. That's a very large loss for an operation your size, and generating it deliberately would cost you far more in real cash than the tax it saves.

Which is the actual trap. If the plan drifts toward buying equipment or inventory in December to manufacture deductions, you're spending a dollar to save thirty-five cents. Section 179 and bonus depreciation are genuinely useful — but only on things the farm needs anyway, where you're accelerating timing on a purchase you'd already make. A high-income year is the right year to *take* that timing. It's the wrong year to invent expenses.

The honest version of the strategy: capture every legitimate expense you're already incurring, document participation hours starting now rather than in April, and pull forward capital purchases already on the list. That's real money. The bracket target isn't the goal — it's a number that happens or doesn't.
