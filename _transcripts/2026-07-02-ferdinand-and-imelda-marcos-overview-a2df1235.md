### CLAUDE (2026-07-02T02:03)
Ferdinand and Imelda Marcos were one of the most consequential — and notorious — political couples in modern history.

**Ferdinand Marcos (1917–1989)** was President of the Philippines from 1965 to 1986. He was elected democratically but consolidated power over time, and in 1972 he declared martial law, suspending the constitution and ruling by decree. His regime became known for authoritarian control, widespread human rights abuses (thousands of documented cases of torture, disappearances, and killings), and large-scale corruption. Estimates suggest he and his associates embezzled somewhere between $5 billion and $10 billion from the country.

**Imelda Marcos (born 1929)** was his wife and a powerful political figure in her own right — she held official positions including Governor of Metro Manila and Minister of Human Settlements. She became a global symbol of excess, most famously for her enormous collection of shoes (around 3,000 pairs) discovered after they fled. She cultivated an image of glamour and grand cultural projects while much of the population lived in poverty.

Their rule ended in 1986 with the **People Power Revolution**, a largely peaceful mass uprising that forced them into exile in Hawaii after a disputed election and military defections. Ferdinand died in exile in 1989.

A notable coda: the family was not permanently exiled from Philippine politics. Imelda returned and served in Congress, and their son, **Ferdinand "Bongbong" Marcos Jr.**, was elected President of the Philippines in 2022 — a remarkable political rehabilitation of the family name.

The Marcos era remains deeply contested in the Philippines, with ongoing debate over historical memory, accountability for martial-law abuses, and recovery of hidden wealth.

Duterte left office in 2022 with approval ratings that were among the highest of any Philippine president in the modern era, often in the 70–80%+ range. So she's not an outlier; she's expressing what a large majority of her countrymen felt.

It's worth understanding *why*, because the reasons are real and not just propaganda:

The strongest case his supporters make is that he delivered a felt sense of order and competence. Crime felt lower. He pushed big infrastructure ("Build, Build, Build"), expanded free tertiary education, passed a universal healthcare law, and was decisive in a system people often see as gridlocked and elite-captured. For many ordinary Filipinos, especially outside Manila, that's concrete and personal. He also *talked* like them — crude, anti-establishment, contemptuous of the Manila elite and the Catholic hierarchy — which read as authenticity.

The counterweight is the thing she may weigh very differently than a Western observer would: the drug war killings. Where critics see thousands of extrajudicial executions of mostly poor people — the exact charges now before the ICC — many supporters saw a necessary, even heroic, hard hand against a genuine drug problem. That's not ignorance of the killings; it's often a different moral judgment about them. A lot of Filipinos will say plainly that they knew people were dying and supported it anyway because they felt safer.

So if you're actually debating her, the unproductive move is treating it as "she doesn't know the facts." She probably knows them. The real disagreement is about values — order and decisive strength versus due process and the sanctity of individual lives, including guilty ones. That's a much harder and more honest conversation, and it's the same fault line running through strongman-populism debates everywhere, not something unique to her or to the Philippines.

One genuinely open question you could put to her, if you want to test the reasoning rather than the sentiment: did the drug war actually *reduce* drug use and crime in a measurable, durable way, or did it mostly produce the *feeling* of safety plus a body count? The evidence on actual outcomes is far weaker than the popularity would suggest — and that gap between perceived and real results is exactly the thing the approval numbers can't tell you.
