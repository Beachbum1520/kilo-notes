### CLAUDE (2026-05-06T12:42)
 Quick things to try, in order:

1. **Restart the hearing aid** — open and close the battery door (or put it in the charger for 10 seconds, then back out). This is the fix about 70% of the time.
2. **Check the wax guard and dome/receiver** — a clogged wax filter on the left side is the most common cause of "connected but silent." If the white filter looks dark or full, replace it with a fresh one from your Widex wax guard pack (NanoCare or CeruStop, depending on your model).
3. **Check the receiver wire** — if you have a RIC (receiver-in-canal) model, gently inspect where the thin wire meets the hearing aid body and where it meets the dome. A loose or damaged receiver will go silent even though Bluetooth stays connected.
4. **In the Widex app** — tap the volume slider and confirm the left side isn't muted independently. Also try switching programs (My Sound) and back.
5. **Forget and re-pair** — Menu → hearing aid settings → remove device, then re-pair both. Connection status can lie when one side has dropped audio.

If wax guard replacement and a restart don't bring it back, it's most likely the receiver itself — those are a wear part and your audiologist can swap one in a few minutes, often same-day. Worth calling them before assuming anything bigger is wrong.

Which model are you on (Moment, SmartRIC, Allure)? That'll narrow down the wax guard type if you need to grab one.

### CLAUDE (2026-05-06T12:43)
 Then it's almost certainly the receiver itself — that's the next failure point after the wax guard on a Moment, and "Bluetooth connects fine but no sound" is the textbook symptom.

A couple more things to rule out before you commit to a service visit:

1. **Swap the dome** — pull the dome off and inspect. Sometimes a dome cracks or seats poorly and muffles output to silence. Even better: swap the left and right domes if they're the same size, and see if the problem follows the dome or stays on the left side.
2. **Swap the receiver wire side-to-side** — if you're comfortable doing it, the RIC wires on Moment are user-replaceable (they pull off the hearing aid body with a gentle tug straight up). Put the right receiver on the left aid. If the left aid now works, the receiver is dead. If the left aid is still silent, the problem is in the hearing aid body itself.
3. **Factory reset via the app** — Menu → My hearing aids → there's usually a reset option. Worth a shot, won't hurt.

If the receiver swap confirms a dead receiver, your audiologist can replace it in about 5 minutes — Widex sells them as a stock part (they come in different power levels and lengths, so don't try to order one yourself unless you know your exact spec from your fitting paperwork). Should be covered under warranty if you're inside the 3-year window from purchase.

If swapping the receiver to the other aid still produces sound on the right side, then the body of the left aid has an internal fault — that's a send-back-to-Widex repair, usually 5–7 business days, and again warranty-covered if applicable.
