Only tradeoff worth knowing:

A hot shower immediately after cuts short the extended cold response. After you get out, your core keeps dropping for a bit (the afterdrop), and that continued shivering/norepinephrine bump is where a lot of the metabolic and adaptation benefit lives. Rewarming fast with hot water blunts that tail.

So if you want the full effect: towel off, dress, let yourself rewarm naturally for ~10–15 min, then shower whenever. If you're crunched and just need to be clean and moving, plunge → hot shower is totally fine — you still get the acute hit, just less of the afterburn.

One thing not to do: hot shower *before* the plunge on a rushed morning, since you'd be dumping the vasodilation right before cold exposure and it makes the plunge feel harsher for no benefit.

Given your day, I'd plunge, dress, knock out a task or two, then shower — rewarming happens while you're productive anyway.
