### CLAUDE (2026-05-10T01:50)
 Creatine is one of the better-supported supplements for women in menopause. Here's the short version:

**What the evidence supports:**
- **Muscle and strength**: Estrogen decline accelerates sarcopenia. Creatine (3-5g/day) combined with resistance training helps preserve lean mass and strength better than training alone.
- **Bone**: Some evidence (notably Candow's work) suggests creatine + resistance training may help slow bone mineral density loss in postmenopausal women, though results are mixed.
- **Cognition and mood**: Emerging data on cognitive performance, fatigue, and even depressive symptoms — likely related to brain energy metabolism. Effects are modest but real.
- **Recovery and sleep deprivation**: Helps buffer cognitive decline from poor sleep, which is relevant given menopausal sleep disruption.

**Practical:**
- 3-5g/day of plain creatine monohydrate. No loading needed for general health goals.
- Timing doesn't matter much. With food slightly improves uptake.
- Brand: anything with Creapure on the label is the gold standard for purity. Optimum Nutrition, Thorne, Klean Athlete are all reliable.

**Caveats worth knowing:**
- Mild water retention in muscle (1-2 lbs) is normal and not fat.
- If she has kidney disease, check with her doctor first. Otherwise the safety profile is excellent — it's one of the most studied supplements in existence.
- Benefits are amplified by resistance training. Without it, you still get some cognitive/recovery benefit but lose most of the muscle/bone upside.

Given Angie's current situation (kidney stone, nephrostomy, pending PCNL, active infection), I'd hold off on starting it until she's fully recovered and cleared by her urologist. Creatine itself doesn't cause kidney stones, but anything that shifts hydration and renal load is worth running by her doc while she has an active stone and tube in place.
