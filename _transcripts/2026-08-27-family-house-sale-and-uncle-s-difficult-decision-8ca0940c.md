### CLAUDE (2026-08-27T22:07)

Sending something is a good idea — it's a small gesture that costs you nothing and probably means a lot to a guy who just made a tough call. But I'd tighten the message. A few thoughts:

**Cut the apology for Lorne.** You're right that the house wasn't kept up, but apologizing *for* your brother puts you in the middle of something that isn't yours to own, and it can read as you piling on right when your uncle is already feeling guilty about selling his parents' house. Your uncle knows exactly how the house was kept — he doesn't need you to confirm it, and Lorne may see this message someday.

**Validate the decision, not the grievance.** The value in your message is "I know this was hard, I get why you did it, you're not wrong for doing it." That's the part your uncle actually needs to hear. Everything about Lorne's housekeeping is old news to him and doesn't change anything now.

**Watch the Ethan angle.** If you want to be genuinely useful, offering help finding Ethan a place (or just acknowledging that part) probably lands better than anything about the yard.

A tighter version:

> "Hey [uncle], heard about the house. I know that wasn't an easy call given everything Grandma and Grandpa built it for, and Dad living there after. For what it's worth, I think you made the right decision. Let me know if there's anything I can do to help Ethan find a place."

Short, respects his position, doesn't drag Lorne through it, and leaves the door open if you actually want to help with Ethan's situation.
