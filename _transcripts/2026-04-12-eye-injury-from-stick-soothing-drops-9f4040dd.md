I took a stick to eye. Hurts like hell. Don’t think there is anything in there. But it hurts like hell. What type of drops can I put in there to soothe it?

### CLAUDE (2026-04-12T21:14)
 That sounds really painful — eye injuries need some care, even if nothing is visibly stuck in there.

**For immediate relief, you can use:**

- **Preservative-free artificial tears** (like Refresh, Systane, or TheraTears) — these are the safest OTC option. They lubricate and flush the eye without causing irritation.
- **Saline eye wash** (like Bausch + Lomb Eye Wash) — good for flushing if you're worried about debris.

**Avoid:**
- Visine or any "redness relief" drops — the vasoconstrictors can mask symptoms and irritate a scratched cornea
- Contact lenses until fully healed
- Rubbing the eye

**Watch for these warning signs that mean go to urgent care or ER now:**
- Vision changes or blurriness
- Significant light sensitivity
- Feeling like something is still in there
- Pain that worsens rather than improves over the next few hours
- Eye won't stop watering or stays shut involuntarily

A stick to the eye can cause a **corneal abrasion**, which is quite common and usually heals in 24–48 hours — but it can also be more serious. If the pain isn't noticeably better in a few hours, an urgent care clinic can do a quick fluorescein dye exam to check for scratches and prescribe antibiotic drops if needed.

### CLAUDE (2026-04-12T21:34)
 If it feels like something is still in there, **don't try to rub it out** — that can scratch your cornea badly.

**Do this right now:**

1. **Flush it** — use clean running water, saline eye wash, or artificial tears. Tilt your head with the affected eye down and let water run from the inner corner outward for a full 60–90 seconds.

2. **Pull your upper eyelid over your lower lash line** — this can dislodge debris using your lower lashes to sweep the underside of the upper lid.

3. **Look in a mirror in good light** — have someone help if possible. Pull down your lower lid and look for visible debris.

**If flushing doesn't clear the sensation within 15–20 minutes, stop trying to self-treat and go get seen.** A foreign body sensation that persists after flushing usually means:
- A small particle is embedded in the cornea or under the lid
- There's already a corneal abrasion causing that "something in there" feeling even if the debris is gone

**This warrants urgent care or an ER today** — they have the tools (slit lamp, fluorescein dye) to find and safely remove embedded debris. A corneal foreign body left in place can cause infection or scarring.

Don't tough this one out, Scott. Eye injuries move fast when they go wrong.
