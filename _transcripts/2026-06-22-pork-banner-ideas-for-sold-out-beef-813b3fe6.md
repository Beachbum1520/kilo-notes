### CLAUDE (2026-06-22T21:13)
Honestly, probably not — at least not a code.

A few reasons it doesn't fit your operation:

Your whole brand runs on "buy our story, not just our beef." Discount codes pull in the opposite direction — they train people to wait for the next deal and they attract price-shoppers, who are the worst-fit customers for a relationship-driven farm. The folks who buy from you aren't choosing you over Kroger to save a buck.

Your beef proved the model already. You moved 14 of 16 halves with zero ad spend and no discounts — pure organic demand and trust. That's the playbook. Pork should lean on the same thing, not a different one.

Codes also eat margin you're still working to build. You've operated at a loss historically and are actively converting to profit. A discount only makes sense if the alternative is freezer-burn or a processing/storage cost you're eating anyway. Is that the situation? If the pork's fresh and freezer life is fine, you don't have a "move it now or lose it" problem — you have a "tell people it's here" problem, which is a marketing fix, not a price fix.

What I'd do instead to move volume without cutting price:

The single most effective lever is a curated box at a built-in "value" — not a code, just a bundle priced so the per-pound feels like a deal because they're buying variety. That moves multiple cuts per order and clears odd inventory (this is exactly what your Bacon & Brat / Brat Sampler / Slow Cook box concepts were built for). It reads as generous, not desperate.

Second, email your list. Warm buyers, personal note from you and Angie, "freezer's full, beef's out til fall, here's what's good right now." That costs nothing and converts better than any code.

If you genuinely have a perishability or storage-cost reason to dump inventory fast, tell me and I'll change my answer — that's the one scenario where a short, framed markdown (not a public code) earns its keep. What's driving the urge to move it — slow sales, freezer space, or cash flow?
