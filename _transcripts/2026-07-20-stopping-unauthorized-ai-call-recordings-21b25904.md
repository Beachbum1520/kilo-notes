### CLAUDE (2026-07-20T14:09)
Here's the cleaned-up draft. I kept the warning understated — it lands in the last line without making a threat you'd have to back up later. One thing I'd flag: since Josh and German are CGO and CEO, asking for an officer-level signature is asking them to sign it themselves, which is fine, but I made that explicit so there's no ambiguity about who owns the response. I also tightened "as my leader has expressed" — it puts Jady's weight behind this without naming her into an action item.

A few notes on the choices:

**The veiled warning** is the final paragraph — "continued lapses will factor into how we evaluate the relationship going forward." It's forward-looking and evaluative rather than threatening, but any executive reading it understands what it means. It also creates a written marker you can point back to later if this ends up in the broader compliance track, without tipping anything about SLA credits or breach leverage.

**"Repeated direction"** — this phrasing quietly establishes the pattern on record (multiple prior communications, leadership involvement) without cataloguing dates. If you *do* have dates of prior communications on this, consider adding one line ("most recently on [date]") — it strengthens the record if this MOU request goes unanswered.

**The deadline** ("confirm by end of week") gives you a clean, dated non-response to escalate on if they slow-roll it. Without it, "we're working on it" can drag indefinitely.

One consideration before sending: given where things stand with CTC overall, you may want to give Jady a heads-up that this is going out — not for approval, since the recording policy is already established with her backing, but so she's not surprised if German calls her. That's consistent with briefing her before CTC escalates.

The instinct to flag it is right — a stale or wrong OOO from the CGO, on this thread of all threads, is worth noting. But the current draft has a problem: the "June 23rd of 2027" line reads as sarcasm, and sarcasm is the one tone that can turn your very clean, factual recording email into something German can characterize as hostile. You wrote the original to be forwardable; this reply would sit on the same thread and dilute it.

The near-certain explanation is a typo for July 23 or a stale auto-reply, so the question mostly answers itself. What's actually useful here isn't the clarification — it's the opening it gives you. Josh being out means German now owns the response, and you can say that plainly rather than letting the request float for a week. That's the better use of this email.

Given the timeline in my note below, please treat this as with you directly — I'd appreciate your confirmation on both items by end of week as requested. This does three things your draft doesn't: it keeps the end-of-week clock running instead of letting the OOO reset it, it puts the deliverable squarely on German, and the "who is covering" question makes the same point your sarcasm was making — that a broken OOO from their CGO is itself a small data point about account attention — without saying it. The dry version of the observation is already on the record just by quoting the dates.

If Josh's departure turns out to be something more than PTO (Kyle Finlinson's exit comes to mind as precedent), you'll hear it in how German answers the coverage question.
