## Suggested layout when reassembling

The end state should look like this, from pump going toward pool:

```
PUMP 
  ↓
[Threaded port on pump]
  ↓
[Union male thread end — screwed into pump with Teflon + dope]
  ↓
[Union body with collar]
  ↓
[Union slip end — glued to short PVC stub]
  ↓
[Short PVC stub — 4-6 inches]
  ↓
[90° elbow above ground — the one you replaced previously]
  ↓
[Vertical pipe going down into the ground]
  ↓
[NEW 90° elbow underground]
  ↓
[Horizontal pipe to skimmer]
```

A few configuration notes:

1. **Position the union close to the pump.** 4-6 inches between the pump and the union is plenty. Don't put it further away — the whole point is easy pump access.

2. **Orient the union collar so it's reachable.** When you glue the union body in, think about how you'll grip the collar to loosen it. Don't bury it behind another fitting or against a wall.

3. **Keep the new elbow at the same orientation as the old one.** Below-ground geometry should match what you remove — same depth, same direction — so you don't introduce new stresses elsewhere in the line.

## Order of operations

Here's the sequence that minimizes redo work:

**Stage 1: Dig and inspect (do this first)**
1. Plug the skimmer
2. Disconnect the suction line at the threaded adapter (unscrew, leaves you with pipe-stub + adapter as one piece)
3. Dig down to the buried elbow
4. Inspect — is the elbow actually cracked?

**If elbow is cracked:** proceed with full repair as planned.

**If elbow is fine:** stop. Re-evaluate. Don't install the union just for the sake of installing it if the elbow wasn't the leak — the leak is somewhere else (skimmer, pad-side, pump itself) and you should diagnose that first before committing to the union install. The union install adds work; only do it if you're already disturbing the line for a real repair.

**Stage 2: Cut out the bad elbow**
1. Cut the pipe 4-6 inches above the buried elbow on the vertical side
2. Cut the pipe 4-6 inches past the elbow on the horizontal side
3. Remove the old elbow and the small pipe stubs

**Stage 3: Plan the rebuild**
1. Dry-fit everything first — never glue without a successful dry-fit
2. New elbow + new short stubs on each side + repair couplings to connect to the existing buried pipe
3. Verify the geometry matches the original (same depth, same direction)

**Stage 4: Glue the underground side**
1. Prime and glue: existing pipe → repair coupling → short stub → new elbow → short stub → repair coupling → existing pipe on the other side
2. Work one joint at a time
3. Let cure at least 2 hours before disturbing

**Stage 5: Install the union at the pump**
1. Apply Teflon + thread sealant to the union's male threads
2. Thread into pump hand-tight, then 1-1.5 turns with strap wrench. **Stop there.**
3. Cut a short PVC stub to bridge from the union's slip end to your above-ground elbow (the one you replaced previously)
4. Prime and glue the slip end and the stub
5. Let cure

**Stage 6: Reconnect and test**
1. Pre-fill the suction line with garden hose (from the pump end, with skimmer still plugged)
2. Tighten the union collar hand-tight
3. Remove skimmer plug
4. Fill pump basket
5. Start pump — should prime quickly
6. Watch for leaks at all new joints
7. Run for 30 minutes — check for the air bubbling at the SWG cell. If clear, you've fixed it. If still bubbling, the leak was somewhere else.

## One judgment call

**Compression repair couplings vs glued slip couplings underground.**

Glued is cheaper and traditional, but requires you to:
- Get the pipe ends perfectly clean and dry (hard underground)
- Have enough pipe flex to slide standard slip couplings on
- Work in tight quarters with primer and glue

Compression repair couplings (look up "PVC compression repair coupling 1.5") solve all three problems:
- No glue, no primer
- Slide on, tighten the compression nuts, done
- Work fine in wet conditions
- Slightly more expensive ($10-20 each)

For a buried elbow repair in a hole in the dirt, I'd genuinely recommend the compression couplings. They're more forgiving and the install is faster. The slight extra cost is worth it for not having to wrestle with glue in a muddy hole.

If the elbow is cracked, then order everything from the full list above.

Don't pre-buy parts for a repair you might not need to do.
