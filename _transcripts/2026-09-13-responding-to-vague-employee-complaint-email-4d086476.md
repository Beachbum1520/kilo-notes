### CLAUDE (2026-09-13T18:46)
Probably not worth a reply — at least not one that engages with the substance.

Here's why: this isn't a customer complaint about an interaction with your business. It's a "kid from school reporting playground gossip about a classmate's mom/sibling's job" situation, filtered through an anonymous contact form. Red flags:

- No actual incident, date, or interaction described — just "she's on her phone and has a bad work ethic," a generalized character claim
- Source is secondhand ("from personal experiences" — not the sender's own experience with your business)
- Sent to a business contact form, not delivered to Sarah's manager directly or through you as employer with standing to know
- No way to verify identity or motive — could be legitimate, could be a school rivalry playing out somewhere it doesn't belong

If you reply at all, keep it minimal and non-committal — something like acknowledging receipt without validating the claim or promising action ("Thanks for reaching out — I'll pass this along"). Don't invite further correspondence or ask Layne for specifics; you don't want to turn a random inbox message into an ongoing thread, and you don't owe an outside party an explanation of how you manage employees.

Separately — worth handling internally regardless of whether the complaint has merit: is Sarah new enough that a normal check-in on phone use / customer-facing manner is due anyway? That's a management conversation with her, independent of this email's credibility.
