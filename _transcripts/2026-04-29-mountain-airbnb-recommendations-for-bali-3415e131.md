### CLAUDE (2026-04-29T16:30)
 Totally reasonable concern, and easy to plan around. Bali sits in a seismically active zone, but the practical risk for a short trip is low — and you can essentially eliminate it by where you sleep.

**The geography that matters:**

Bali's tsunami risk is concentrated on the south and southwest coasts (Kuta, Seminyak, Canggu, Jimbaran) because they face the Java Trench. The east coast (Sanur, Candidasa, Amed) faces the Lombok Strait and has historically seen less activity. Anywhere inland or elevated is essentially safe — tsunamis don't climb mountains.

**Options that solve this:**

*Stay inland the whole trip.* Munduk (~800m elevation) and Ubud area (~200–600m, 25km from coast) are both well out of any tsunami zone. You can still do beach day trips — the risk window during a few daylight hours at the beach is vanishingly small, and warning systems would give time to move to higher ground.

*Pick elevated beach areas.* **Uluwatu** is on cliffs 50–100m above the ocean. The beaches are reached by stairs down the cliff face, and the villas/hotels sit on top. You get world-class beach access with built-in elevation. This is probably your best compromise if she wants beach time but feels safer up high.

*Stay east, not south.* **Sanur** is calmer water, lower historical risk than the southwest, and has a more protected reef. **Amed** on the far east coast is quiet and faces away from the main subduction zone.

**What I'd actually suggest for your 5 days:**

Munduk (mountains, zero coastal exposure) → Uluwatu (cliff-top villa, beach access without sleeping at sea level). She gets mountain immersion and you both get beach time without sleeping somewhere that would worry her.

Worth also noting: Indonesia has an active tsunami warning system (sirens, SMS alerts), and any decent hotel/villa briefs guests on evacuation routes. But the elevation solution is simpler than relying on that.
