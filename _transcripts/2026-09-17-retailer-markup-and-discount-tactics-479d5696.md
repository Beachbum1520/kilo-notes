### CLAUDE (2026-09-17T16:22)

Yes — classic anchor-and-discount, and there's a specific tell here that confirms it: this exact refrigerator (Model # LHFS28XBS, "Sold Exclusively at The Home Depot") is an HD-exclusive SKU. It's sold exclusively at Home Depot, and a nearly identical listing for the same model shows it at "$1,799, was $2,799" — different discount math, different "was" price, same item, same store, same week.

That's the mechanism: when a manufacturer builds a model number exclusively for one retailer, there's no competing seller to arbitrage the price against. The retailer sets both the "was" (fabricated MSRP-equivalent) and the "now" price, so the "35% off" is theater — you can't check it against Best Buy or Lowe's because the SKU doesn't exist anywhere else. Two listings of the same fridge showing different "regular" prices ($3,099 vs $2,799) is direct evidence the anchor is arbitrary, not a real market price that got marked down.

Does that mean $1,999 is a bad price? Not necessarily — LG French-door units with Craft Ice in this capacity class do typically transact in the $1,900–$2,400 range depending on finish and door count, so this isn't wildly out of line. But you're not "saving $1,100" — you're paying roughly market rate and being sold a manufactured sense of urgency. Two moves that actually matter more than the sticker theater:

- Check if this is a 3-door or 4-door — the two Home Depot search hits use "3 Door" and "4-Door" for what look like overlapping product families; confirm which one your screenshot's SKU actually is before buying.
- Ignore the "Was" price entirely and price-check the model number (LHFS28XBS or whatever yours resolves to) against nothing, because it won't resolve anywhere else — that's the point. Your real comparison should be against other brands' actual cu-ft/feature equivalents (Samsung, Whirlpool, GE) at real multi-retailer prices, not against HD's own invented anchor.
