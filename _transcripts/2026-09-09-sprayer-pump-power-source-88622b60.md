Based on what's in the photos, I can see the tank, boom poles, hoses, and a valve assembly, but I can't see an actual pump or motor housing clearly in either shot — no gas engine is visible, and I don't see a PTO driveshaft either.

Most skid/trailer sprayers built like this (poly tank, folding boom, hose reel, that blue frame) run on one of two setups:

- **12V electric diaphragm pump** — wired straight to a tractor, truck, or ATV battery. This is by far the most common setup on tanks this size, and I don't see anything that looks like a gas engine or PTO shaft in your pics, which points this way.
- **PTO-driven pump** — would need a driveshaft running off a tractor's PTO to a gearbox/pump, then hoses out to the boom. I don't see that hardware in these photos.

If you can get a photo of the actual pump housing (usually a small black box near where the hoses converge, with two wire leads coming off it) or any nameplate/brand sticker, I can tell you exactly what you're powering it with and how to wire it up.

### CLAUDE (2026-09-09T02:33)
It could be — hydraulic-driven pumps are common on ag sprayers too, especially ones meant to mount on a tractor or skid-steer with existing hydraulic outlets. A hydraulic setup would show:

- A hydraulic motor (small cylindrical housing, usually with two hoses — pressure and return — using quick-disconnect couplers)
- Braided hydraulic hose rather than the smooth poly/rubber hose you'd use for water lines
- No wiring at all, since it draws power straight off the tractor's hydraulic system rather than a battery

Looking at the photos again, the hoses running to the pump area look like standard water-transfer/suction hose rather than braided hydraulic line, and I don't see quick-disconnect hydraulic couplers — but the resolution and angle make it hard to be certain either way.

The tell-tale sign either way: hydraulic pumps don't need a battery or wiring nearby, while electric ones will have a wire lead running off toward a battery terminal. If you can check whether there's a battery connection nearby, or get a close-up of the pump housing itself, that'll settle it.
