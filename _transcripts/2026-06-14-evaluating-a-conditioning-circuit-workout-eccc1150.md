### CLAUDE (2026-06-14T16:36)
Right — and that's the right instinct. Picking apart why something works or doesn't is how you build the judgment to coach yourself when I'm not in the loop.

So the useful takeaway from this one: it's a clean example of a workout that's optimized for engagement, not adaptation. The 10×10 structure exists because it's brutal and shareable — a number people can rally around in the comments. That's a different design goal than progressive overload. Once you can spot "is this built to produce a training adaptation or to produce a feeling," you can categorize almost anything that crosses your feed in about five seconds.

The other tell worth internalizing: any program that can't answer "how does this get harder next week" isn't a strength program. Circuits like this have no overload mechanism, so they plateau as conditioning work and then just become a tax. Your RP block has RIR targets and calibration lifts precisely so the question always has an answer.

It's not that the movements are bad — overhead press, squat, row, lunge are all legitimate patterns. It's that load, rest, and progression are the variables that determine what a movement *does*, and this strips all three down to "light, none, never." Same exercises, different dials, completely different outcome.
