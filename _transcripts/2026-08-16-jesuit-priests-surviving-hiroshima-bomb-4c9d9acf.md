### CLAUDE (2026-08-16T10:58)
**Partly true — the survival is real; the "miracle" framing doesn't hold up.**

What's documented:

- Eight German Jesuits at the Church of Our Lady's Assumption in Nobori-cho, roughly 1 km from the hypocenter, survived the blast. That part is solid history.
- Their rectory was still standing, though heavily damaged. It had been built by a Jesuit brother to earthquake-resistant standards and was shielded by surrounding structures.

Where the viral version breaks down:

- **"No radiation effects, examined 200+ times by doctors."** That claim traces back almost entirely to Fr. Hubert Schiffer's own retellings decades later, mostly at Fatima/rosary devotional events. There's no independent medical record supporting it.
- **They were not unharmed.** Fr. Wilhelm Kleinsorge — profiled in John Hersey's *Hiroshima* — developed acute radiation sickness, was repeatedly hospitalized, and suffered poor health for the rest of his life. Hugo Lassalle was cut up by flying glass. Schiffer himself had glass wounds.
- **Survival at 1 km wasn't unique.** Eizo Nomura survived in a basement about 170 meters from the hypocenter. Thousands survived within a kilometer, mostly determined by shielding, building construction, and whether they were indoors. Structures did survive that close — the Genbaku Dome in that photo is the most famous example, and nobody calls it a miracle.

The causal claim being made is that praying the rosary produced physical immunity. The actual explanation is reinforced construction, shielding, and being inside — the same factors that explain every other close-in survivor, including the atheists.
