### CLAUDE (2026-09-11T02:31)
The lung note — "calcified left lower lobe pulmonary granuloma" — is a benign, incidental finding. A granuloma is just a small area of old scar tissue, usually left behind after the body walled off a minor lung infection (histoplasmosis and TB exposure are the classic causes in the Southeast). When it calcifies, that's the body's way of saying "handled, closed case." It's an extremely common finding on abdominal/pelvis CTs that happen to catch the lung bases, and radiologists mention it simply because it's visible, not because it needs follow-up. No mass, no nodule of concern, no recommendation for a chest workup anywhere in the report.

The more clinically active part of this scan is actually the kidney findings: the previously seen 12mm left ureteropelvic junction stone is gone, and the hydronephrosis/hydroureter (swelling from obstruction) has improved — that's good news, meaning the bigger stone passed or resolved. There's a new small 2.0mm nonobstructing stone lower down on the left, plus a tiny 1mm stone on the right — both too small to obstruct and often pass on their own.

Nothing in either image points to anything urgent. If she still has flank pain despite the obstruction clearing, that's worth a follow-up call with the ordering doctor, but the lung note itself is not something to worry about.
