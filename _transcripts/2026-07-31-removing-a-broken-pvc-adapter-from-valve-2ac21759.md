**The hacksaw-blade method (best, free)**

1. Stuff a rag in the port so shards don't drop into the valve.
2. Take a bare hacksaw blade (no frame), teeth toward you, and make two cuts through the remaining PVC ring about 1/2" apart, at roughly 12 and 1 o'clock. Angle the blade so you're cutting the PVC only — stop the instant you feel the valve's threads. You're aiming to get thin, not to cut through.

**Skip these:** screw extractors/easy-outs (they wedge outward and crack the port) and heat guns (softened PVC smears into the threads and you risk warping the valve body).

- Inspect that port under good light. If the original leak came from a hairline crack in the valve body rather than the threads, no new adapter will seal it and you're buying an SP0714T.
