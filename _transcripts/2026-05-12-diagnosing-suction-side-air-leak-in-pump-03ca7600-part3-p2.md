Most decent floor pumps (the kind you stand on to inflate bike tires) have a pressure gauge built into the pump itself. While the pump is connected to the Schrader valve, the gauge reads the pressure in whatever it's connected to — which would be your suction line.

Pros:
- You probably already have one
- Pump and gauge in one tool
- Easy to use

Cons:
- Once you disconnect the pump from the Schrader, you can no longer read pressure
- So you can't "set it and walk away" — you'd have to stay connected to watch the gauge over 15-30 minutes
- Or pressurize, disconnect, come back later, reconnect and read — but reconnecting briefly lets air escape, so the reading is slightly off

**Option 2: Tire pressure gauge on the Schrader valve**

A handheld tire pressure gauge (the kind you press onto a tire valve to check pressure) reads pressure when pressed onto the Schrader valve.

Pros:
- Cheap ($5-10)
- You probably have one for your car tires
- Quick spot checks

Cons:
- Same issue as option 1 — you have to be present to read
- Briefly releases a tiny bit of air each time you check

**Option 3: A gauge that stays connected (best for this test)**

A small pressure gauge that screws onto a tee fitting, with the Schrader on one side and the gauge on the other. Once installed, the gauge reads pressure continuously. You can pump it up, walk away, come back in 30 minutes, and see the reading.

This is what dedicated "pool pressure test kits" include.

Pros:
- Set and forget
- Most accurate
- Can leave it pressurized overnight if needed

Cons:
- Extra part to buy
- More setup complexity

## What I actually recommend

For a one-time test, **use what you already have**. If you have a bike floor pump with a gauge, that's enough. Process:

1. Set up the plugs (skimmer + pump suction inlet plug with Schrader)
2. Connect your bike pump to the Schrader
3. Pump up to 10-15 PSI — watch the bike pump's gauge as you go
4. Stop pumping at target pressure
5. **Keep the pump connected** — don't disconnect
6. Watch the gauge over the next 5-10 minutes

Even just 5 minutes of watching tells you a lot:
- **Gauge holds steady at the pressure you set** → line is airtight, no leak
- **Gauge drops 1-2 PSI in 5 min** → small leak in the line
- **Gauge drops 5+ PSI in 5 min** → significant leak
- **Won't hold pressure at all** → big leak or plug isn't sealing

You don't need to leave it for 30 minutes. The leak we're chasing is significant enough that you'd see meaningful pressure drop within minutes.

## If you don't have a bike pump with gauge

Cheapest path: buy a $20-30 pool pressure test kit that includes the plug, Schrader fitting, and gauge as a single integrated unit. Search Amazon for "pool pressure test kit" — you'll find complete kits.

Or buy them separately:
- Expansion plug with Schrader: $15-20
- Small gauge that threads onto a Schrader fitting: $10-15
- Bike pump (any kind, even handheld): $15-30 if you don't have one
