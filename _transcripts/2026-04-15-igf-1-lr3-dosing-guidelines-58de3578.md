### CLAUDE (2026-04-15T15:38)
 IGF-1 LR3 is a research peptide — here's a practical overview based on what's commonly discussed in the performance/optimization space:

**What it is:** A synthetic analog of IGF-1 with an arginine substitution that reduces binding to IGF-binding proteins, extending half-life from ~12 min (native IGF-1) to ~20–30 hours. More systemic effect than localized IGF-1.

---

**Common dosing ranges**

- **Conservative/introductory:** 20–40 mcg/day
- **Moderate:** 50–80 mcg/day
- **Higher end (bodybuilding context):** 80–120 mcg/day
- **Cycle length:** Typically 4–6 weeks, then a break of equal or greater length (receptor desensitization is real)

---

**Timing considerations**

- Post-workout is the most common recommendation — elevated nutrient partitioning window
- Some protocols split dose (AM + post-workout)
- Fasted administration is debated; most prefer fed state to support anabolic signaling

---

**Key risks and considerations**

- **Hypoglycemia** — most significant acute risk; always have fast carbs on hand, especially early in a protocol
- **Acromegalic effects** with prolonged high-dose use (jaw, organ growth) — this is the long-game concern with any IGF axis peptide
- **Cancer concern** — IGF-1 is a potent mitogen; anyone with family history or elevated cancer risk should think carefully
- **Receptor downregulation** — the case for cycling; continuous use blunts response
- **Interaction with insulin** — if you're using both, hypoglycemia risk multiplies significantly

---

**Practical notes for your context**

Given your endurance/triathlon focus, the case for IGF-1 LR3 is more about recovery and lean tissue preservation than hypertrophy. Lower doses (20–50 mcg) post-workout during a heavier training block could theoretically support faster tissue repair. That said, the risk/benefit calculus is different for an endurance athlete vs. a bodybuilder — you're not chasing mass, so the ceiling on useful dose is lower.

Worth tracking: morning fasting glucose and HRV during any protocol. Glucose creep or HRV suppression would be meaningful signals.

This is research territory — not prescribed or clinically validated for this use. Treat it accordingly.
