### SCOTT (2026-06-22T16:29)
Should i cut the chicken up before I cook it?

### CLAUDE (2026-06-22T16:30)
No — cook it whole, cut after. Three reasons:

1. **Stays juicier.** Whole breast holds moisture; pre-cut pieces shed it and dry out faster — matters a lot when these sit 5+ days and get reheated.
2. **Less overcooking risk.** Small pieces go from done to rubbery fast. A whole breast gives you margin to hit 165°F without zapping it.
3. **Better for meal prep specifically.** Cook whole, rest 5 min, then slice or cube — the rest lets juices redistribute so cubing doesn't bleed them onto the cutting board.

Exception: if you're pan-searing and want speed, butterfly thick breasts (slice horizontally into two thin cutlets) so they cook evenly and fast — but still cut into bite pieces only *after* cooking and resting.

So: whole (or butterflied) → cook to 165°F → rest → then cube.

### SCOTT (2026-06-22T16:31)
Ya. But what about the taco seasoning?

### CLAUDE (2026-06-22T16:31)
Season the whole breast before cooking, not after cutting. The rub goes on raw, right before it hits the pan or oven — that's where it sets a crust and the flavor cooks into the surface.

The catch: cooking whole means the seasoning's only on the outside, so the interior is plainer once you cube it. Two easy fixes:

1. **Toss after cubing.** Cook whole and seasoned, rest, cube, then sprinkle a little more seasoning over the cut pieces and toss. The cut surfaces grab it and every bite gets coated. This is the meal-prep move — best of both.
2. **Splash of liquid.** When you toss the cubes, add a teaspoon of oil or a splash of the pan juices/water so the dry seasoning sticks instead of dusting off.

So: season raw breast → cook to 165°F → rest → cube → light second sprinkle + toss. Salt's in the blend, so go easy on the second pass.
