## Payment terms review — you have time

Neither SOW gives CallTek a payment deadline to hold over you. Specifics:

- **No net terms in either SOW.** Payment due dates would live in the Master Agreement (Aug 18, 2020), which isn't attached here. Neither SOW 2 nor SOW 3 contains any "payment due within X days" language. Two follow-ups in 15 days is vendor cash-flow pressure, not a contractual clock. Worth pulling the Master Agreement to confirm what terms actually apply before assuming any deadline.
- **Section 3.1.3 (both SOWs) — Retroactive Billing cuts against *them*, not you:** invoices submitted more than 3 months after services are performed get denied by BPRF. The only billing deadline in these documents constrains CallTek.
- **SOW 2, Section 5.1.1 — SLA credits must appear on the invoice itself:** "Supplier will credit the SLA penalty amount on the next monthly invoice... shown as a line item for clarity." If there were any May/June SLA misses on the help desk side (85% answer in 30 sec, ≤10 min guest resolution, ≥85% staff classification in 20 min, <5% abandon — penalties of 7/7/5/5% of monthly fees), the June invoice should already carry those deductions. If it doesn't, the invoice is non-conforming on its face — that alone justifies withholding approval.
- **SOW 2, Section 6.1 — billing cap:** max 135 productive hours per FTE against the locked monthly headcount. Audit check: hours billed vs. locked forecast.
- **SOW 3, Section 3.1.1 — pro-ration for open roles:** the 44-FTE roster at $76,820.98 assumes full staffing. Any vacancies in June must be pro-rated. Given the resource instability you've documented, this is a live audit item.
- **Section 3.1.4 (both) — no other costs without an executed Change Order.** If the $2K/month overflow seat charge or anything ad-hoc appears on these invoices, it's not payable absent a Change Order.

## On your draft — I'd cut the pre-approval offer

Your draft concedes you can complete the audit by end of next week. That makes the conditional approval unnecessary — you're offering to trade away your only pre-payment review for roughly five business days of vendor convenience. Two problems with the trade:

1. It puts you on record (wide cc, forwardable) approving un-audited invoices from a vendor you're building a compliance file on. If the audit later finds SLA credits missing or unfilled roles billed, you're chasing a credit instead of holding a payment.
2. "Credit adjustments to a future invoice" is structurally weaker than the SOW 2 mechanism, which requires credits on *this* invoice. You'd be granting them softer terms than they signed.

Suggested reply:

---

All — apologies for the delay here as I attend to some family matters.

These invoices are in queue for audit review, which I expect to complete by end of next week. Per the SOWs, the review will confirm productive hours against the locked headcount (SOW 2, Section 6.1), pro-ration for any open roles (SOW 3, Section 3.1.1), and that any applicable SLA credits are reflected as line items on the invoice as required under SOW 2, Section 5.1.1.

Once the review is complete, I'll confirm approval and you can proceed with the portal upload. I'll reach out directly if anything requires clarification.

Thank you for your patience.

---

This keeps the tone cooperative, gives them a date, and quietly puts the Section references on the record — which pays forward into the August SLA cycle. If you'd rather preserve the goodwill gesture and keep your conditional-approval structure, the minimum protective edit is adding: *"Approval for processing does not constitute acceptance of the charges; BPRF reserves the right to audit and offset any adjustments, including applicable SLA credits, against amounts due."* But my recommendation is the audit-first version — a one-week hold needs no concession.

One more thing before you send: worth a quick heads-up to Jady only if you expect the audit to surface disputes. If it's clean, no briefing needed.

### CLAUDE (2026-07-22T11:05)
The MSA settles it — you have far more runway than you thought, and their follow-up cadence is pure AR pressure with no contractual footing.

**Article 8.2 / 8.3.1 — Net 45 from receipt.** You received the invoices July 6, so payment isn't due until approximately **August 20**. Their July 17 and July 21 follow-ups came 11 and 15 days into a 45-day window. There is nothing to chase yet. Your end-of-next-week audit (~July 31) leaves you roughly three weeks of contractual cushion even after the review completes.

Other provisions worth having in your pocket:

- **8.2 dispute mechanism:** If the audit surfaces issues, you can withhold the disputed portion and pay the undisputed remainder. Written dispute notice with a reasonable description of the basis is due no later than the payment due date (~Aug 20) — so your audit timeline comfortably supports a clean dispute notice if needed. Per **12.2**, CallTek then owes you supporting documentation within 30 days.
- **8.3.3 + Exhibit H:** BPRF may return any invoice that doesn't comply with Invoicing Requirements and has **no obligation to pay until corrected** — including the required credit notes with invoice references. Combined with SOW 2 §5.1.1 (SLA credits must appear as line items on the invoice), a June invoice missing applicable SLA credits is arguably non-conforming on its face and returnable.
- **8.3.1 early-pay discount:** 2% if paid in full within 15 business days of receipt. That window has passed for these invoices, so there's no discount incentive pushing you to move fast.
- **9.4 is their only real lever, and it's distant:** CallTek can't even start a termination clock unless you're 30+ days delinquent on the *undisputed* portion after written notice, plus a 30-day cure. You're nowhere near it.

**Revised recommendation on your reply:** Drop the conditional pre-approval entirely — it concedes leverage you contractually hold for another month. Also, no apology is needed for a "delay" that isn't one. Suggested version:

All — thank you for the follow-up.

These invoices are in queue for audit review, which I expect to complete by end of next week — well within the net-45 payment terms under Section 8.2 of the Master Agreement (invoices received July 6). Once the review is complete, I'll confirm approval and you can proceed with the portal upload. I'll reach out directly if anything requires clarification.

---

Citing net-45 does two things: it politely tells their AR team to stand down through mid-August, and it demonstrates you're operating from the contract — which reads forward into the August SLA cycle and any dispute you may need to notice. The family-matters apology from your draft can stay or go; my read is it invites the sympathy-then-pressure dynamic, and the contract terms make it unnecessary. If the audit does surface SLA credit gaps or unfilled-role billing, that's the point to brief Jady — you'd be issuing a formal dispute notice under 8.2, which fits your established pattern of aligning her before activation.
