Standard hack:

**Kerf-and-collapse (the one that actually works):**
1. Pump off, valve depressurized.
2. With a hacksaw blade held by hand (wrap the back in tape), a Dremel with a cutoff wheel, or an oscillating multi-tool, cut two lengthwise slots through the PVC stub — roughly 10 and 2 o'clock — from the inside face outward.
3. Cut through the PVC wall only. Go slow and stop the instant you feel the bite change; the black valve threads are right behind it.
4. Pry one segment inward at a slot with a screwdriver. It collapses and the rest peels/unthreads out in a spiral.
5. Chase the threads with a nylon brush or plastic pick to clear the old Teflon and PVC crumbs.

The store-bought version is a drill-driven "fitting saver" / inside pipe extractor in 1.5" — about $30, works well if you'd rather not freehand a blade near the threads.

**But check this first.** In the second photo there's a line running through the port hub and down the valve body. Some of what I'm seeing is spider web, but if any of it is an actual crack in the black plastic, that's your leak source — not the fitting — and extraction won't fix it. Over-torqued male adapters split these ports routinely. Wipe it down, dry it, and look at the hub in good light before you invest in the extraction. A replacement SP0714T is around $100–150 and swaps in an afternoon.

When you reinstall: 3 wraps of Teflon tape plus a light smear of Teflon paste, hand tight plus **1 to 1.5 turns max**. That's what kills these ports.
