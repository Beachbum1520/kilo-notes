**The three approved sites and what's actually different about them**

Tirzepatide is approved for subcutaneous injection in three areas: **abdomen, thigh, and upper arm (back of arm/triceps area)**. The pharmacokinetic studies done by Lilly tested all three and concluded "bioequivalence" — meaning the drug eventually reaches similar blood levels regardless of site.

But "bioequivalent over the full week" doesn't mean "identical experience." Here's what differs:

**Absorption rate (fastest to slowest):**
1. Abdomen — fastest absorption, highest peak concentration, most aggressive onset
2. Upper arm — moderate
3. Thigh — slowest absorption, gentler peak, more gradual onset

The total drug absorbed is similar over 7 days (tirzepatide has a ~5-day half-life so absorption rate matters less than for short-acting drugs), but the *peak* concentration in the first 24-48 hours varies meaningfully by site.

**What this means practically**

**If you get strong GI side effects** (nausea, early satiety, food aversion in the first 1-3 days post-injection), the **thigh** is your friend. Slower absorption = lower peak = milder onset. People who switched from abdomen to thigh frequently report 30-50% reduction in peak nausea.

**If you want maximum appetite suppression** in the first 2-3 days post-shot (some people use this strategically for events, weekends, social eating windows), the **abdomen** delivers the strongest hit.

**If you have lean tissue concerns** (relevant for you), there's a small theoretical advantage to the abdomen because subcutaneous fat depot there is the largest and most consistent. Standard advice is "rotate sites to prevent lipohypertrophy" (fatty lumps from repeated injection in the same spot). For weekly injection, this is real but often overstated. You're injecting 52 times a year, not multiple times daily like a Type 1 diabetic.

- Don't inject the same exact spot week after week — vary by at least an inch within the same general area
- If you do rotate sites (abdomen one week, thigh the next), expect a slightly different experience week to week. Some people find this disorienting; others don't notice
