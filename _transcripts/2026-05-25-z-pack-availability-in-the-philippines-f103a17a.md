### CLAUDE (2026-05-25T13:26)
 Yes, Z-Packs (azithromycin) are absolutely available in the Philippines — sold under the brand name Zithromax as well as numerous generic versions. It's actually easier to get there than in the US in some ways, since many antibiotics are available over the counter at pharmacies (Mercury Drug, Watsons, Rose Pharmacy) without a prescription, though that practice varies by location and pharmacist.

A few honest pushbacks before you go that route though:

**Chest cold ≠ bacterial infection.** The vast majority of chest colds are viral — azithromycin does nothing against viruses. Using it won't speed recovery and contributes to antibiotic resistance. The Filipino medical community (and WHO) has been pushing back on the OTC antibiotic culture for exactly this reason.

**When azithromycin actually makes sense:** bacterial bronchitis with purulent (yellow/green) sputum persisting beyond 7–10 days, atypical pneumonia (Mycoplasma, Chlamydophila), or if you're running a fever and feeling significantly worse rather than plateau/improving.

**What actually works for a viral chest cold:** rest, hydration, mucolytics (guaifenesin — Robitussin, widely available), and time. If congestion is bad, pseudoephedrine or a nasal corticosteroid.

**Your specific situation:** You're 55, active, and otherwise healthy — not high-risk. But if you're heading to Bali soon, you don't want this dragging. If symptoms are worsening after day 5–7, or you develop fever/chest pain/significant shortness of breath, that's when getting evaluated (and possibly a Z-Pack) is warranted.

Bottom line: hold off on the azithromycin unless you're clearly bacterial. Grab guaifenesin and give it a few days.

### CLAUDE (2026-05-25T15:24)
 Fisherman's Friend Original — good instinct. That's actually one of the more legitimate throat/cough lozenges out there, not just candy with menthol branding.

**What's in it that matters:** high-dose menthol (10mg per lozenge) plus eucalyptus oil. The menthol acts as a topical anesthetic on throat receptors and a counterirritant that suppresses cough reflex. It also creates the sensation of easier breathing by stimulating cold receptors — doesn't actually open airways, but the relief is real and useful.

**Honest assessment for your situation:** Good for symptomatic relief on the flight — suppresses cough, soothes irritated throat, helps with the dry cabin air misery. Won't treat the underlying infection but that's not what you need it for right now.

The "Extra Strong" designation is real — these are noticeably more aggressive than the knockoffs or the milder flavored versions. First one catches people off guard. You'll know it's working.

**One note:** don't chain them constantly. The menthol in high doses can actually irritate mucous membranes if you're going through a bag a day. A few spread through the flight is the right use.

Solid Bali airport find. Pair it with guaifenesin and water on the flight and you're doing everything right symptomatically. Safe travels to Manila.
