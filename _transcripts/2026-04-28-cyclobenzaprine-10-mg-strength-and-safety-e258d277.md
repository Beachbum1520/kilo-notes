### CLAUDE (2026-04-28T21:48)
 Cyclobenzaprine 10 mg is a moderate-strength dose — it's the most common starting dose and the standard adult strength.

A few things worth knowing:

**On the dose itself:** 5 mg and 10 mg are the two immediate-release strengths. 10 mg three times daily is the typical max for IR. Many clinicians actually start at 5 mg now because 10 mg knocks a lot of people out. Your label says "as needed" twice daily, which is a reasonable conservative use.

**What to expect:** It's a centrally-acting muscle relaxant (structurally related to tricyclic antidepressants, which is why it's sedating). Drowsiness, dry mouth, and dizziness are the big three side effects. The sedation can be significant — first dose, take it somewhere you don't need to drive, operate equipment, or make decisions for a few hours.

**Watch out for:**
- Don't combine with alcohol, opioids, benzos, or sleep aids — additive CNS depression
- Hangover effect is real; people often feel groggy the next morning
- Most effective short-term (2–3 weeks); it loses utility for chronic use
- If you're on anything serotonergic (SSRIs, tramadol, etc.), there's a small serotonin syndrome risk worth flagging to your prescriber

Given everything on your plate right now — farm work, kids, hospital runs — I'd be cautious about timing the first dose. Don't take it before driving anywhere or handling cattle/equipment until you know how it hits you.
