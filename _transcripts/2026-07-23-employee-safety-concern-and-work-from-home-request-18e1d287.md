### CLAUDE (2026-07-23T18:55)
This one is different from the pattern, and I'd treat it that way. A few things stand out:

**The corroboration is real.** The screenshots aren't a doctor's note she could have manufactured. She contacted the condo manager the night of the incident asking for guards to remove her brother from the unit ("nanghasi" = threatened/menaced), guards were dispatched, and the manager confirmed removal. The earlier message about CCTV footage requiring a police request suggests she asked about pulling footage too. That's a contemporaneous record consistent with her account, not an excuse assembled after the fact.

**The risk asymmetry is heavily against denial.** If you deny WFH after an employee reports a knife-point death threat and anything happens to her — or she resigns and characterizes the denial — that's an ugly record for you, BPRF, and the BPO relationship. The cost of granting two weeks of WFH to a high-productivity performer is near zero. The cost of being the manager who made her commute past a documented safety threat is not. Everything you write here should be assumed forwardable to Cloudstaff HR, Jady, or worse.

**Separate the incident from the pattern — explicitly and deliberately.** Your instinct that she games WFH may be right. This is still the wrong vehicle to litigate it. If you bundle skepticism about her history into a response to a death threat, you lose the moral and documentary high ground on both issues. Grant this one cleanly, then address the chronic pattern later in a normal performance/policy conversation where the facts favor you.

**Structure the grant so it doesn't become open-ended:**
- Time-boxed: e.g., WFH approved through a specific date (1–2 weeks), with a check-in before extension. "For the mean time" is exactly the ambiguity you don't want.
- Route it through her employer of record's HR (Cloudstaff or CTC, whichever holds her). They have duty-of-care obligations, and you want their HR on record as informed. This also protects you — it's their employee-safety process, not your ad hoc exception.
- Encourage (don't require) a police blotter or barangay report. It protects her, and it also creates the documentation that distinguishes this from a soft excuse if it recurs.
- Ask about what would make her feel safe returning — guard awareness is already in place; that gives you a natural bridge back to WFO rather than an indefinite drift.

**One caution on tone:** she's disclosed trauma and anxiety. Lead with concern for her safety, keep it short, and don't ask her to justify further — the proof she volunteered is already more than most would provide. Skepticism, even implied, will read badly in writing.
