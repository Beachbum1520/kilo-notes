### CLAUDE (2026-05-23T06:43)
 That's a **Eastern Ratsnake** (*Pantherophis alleghaniensis*) — almost certainly. Here's my read:

**Supporting ID factors:**
- Solid dark/charcoal-gray dorsal coloring with a slightly lighter underside visible at the curves
- Slender, elongated body — this one looks 4–5+ feet
- Smooth, slightly keeled scales
- Active, fluid movement through ground cover
- Franklin, GA habitat is textbook ratsnake territory

**Not a cottonmouth** — key differentiators: no blocky triangular head visible, body is too slender, no banding pattern, and cottonmouths are typically heavier-bodied and slower/more defensive. Cottonmouths also tend to hold their ground and gape rather than move like this one appears to be doing.

**Not a black racer either**, though close. Racers are more uniformly jet black and have a more uniform round cross-section. This one's coloring and slight keeling lean ratsnake.

**Farm context:** This is actually a net positive for Watts Way Farms. Ratsnakes are rodent control assets — they'll hit your feed room before any cottonmouth would. The tradeoff is they'll also raid nests (chicken eggs, bird nests).

**Bond Arms relevance:** Still warranted for the cottonmouth population you know you have. This one doesn't need a .410 — just space. But you were right to document it; knowing your non-venomous population helps you calibrate threat assessment on the fly.
