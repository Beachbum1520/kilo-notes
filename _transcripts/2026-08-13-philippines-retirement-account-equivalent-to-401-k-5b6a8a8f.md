### CLAUDE (2026-08-13T01:35)

**PERA** — Personal Equity and Retirement Account, created under RA 9505 (PERA Act of 2008). It's the standard answer, though structurally it's closer to a Roth IRA than a 401(k).

How it works:Contribution caps are ₱200,000/year for locally employed and self-employed individuals, ₱400,000/year for overseas Filipinos. You get a 5% tax credit on qualified contributions (₱10,000 max locally, ₱20,000 for OFWs), and investment income inside the account is tax-exempt. Withdrawals at 55+ are tax free. It's a wrapper, not a fund — you hold PERA-eligible UITFs, mutual funds, bonds, or stocks inside it.

Where the 401(k) analogy breaks down:
- **Voluntary, not employer-driven.** Individuals may open accounts independently or through employer-led arrangements, but employer-sponsored PERA is rare — the BSP was still pushing employers to adopt it as recently as July 2026.
- **No real match culture.** Employer contributions are allowed but count against the same annual cap, so they crowd out employee contributions rather than adding on top.
- **The mandatory layer is separate.** SSS (private sector) / GSIS (government) is the compulsory base, and RA 7641 obligates employers to pay retirement pay at 60-65 — a defined-benefit liability, not a DC account.

So the real stack for a Philippine employee is: SSS + statutory retirement pay + optional PERA. If you're evaluating this as a benefit for offshore staff, the more common private-sector vehicle is actually a company-sponsored **retirement fund under a BIR-qualified plan** (RA 4917), which gets tax-exempt treatment on the employer side and is how larger PH employers handle it.
