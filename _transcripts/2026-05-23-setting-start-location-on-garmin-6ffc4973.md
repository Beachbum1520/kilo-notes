### CLAUDE (2026-05-23T22:11)
 On a Garmin device, "start location" depends on what you're doing:

**For Navigation/Routing:**
1. Press the **Menu** button
2. Select **Where To?** or **Navigation**
3. Choose your destination, then before confirming, look for **Route Preferences** or tap the route line
4. Select **Change Start** or **Edit Route** → set a custom start point

**For Activities (running/cycling):**
- The start location is set automatically by GPS when you press **Start** — just wait for GPS to acquire your position before starting.

**For Courses:**
1. Go to **Navigation → Courses**
2. Select your course → **Do Course**
3. Some devices let you select a starting waypoint within the course

**For Connect IQ / Garmin Connect (phone/web):**
1. Open **Garmin Connect** app
2. Go to your activity or course
3. Tap the map and look for **Edit** or **Recalculate from here**

### CLAUDE (2026-05-23T22:12)
 That's the **Back to Start** feature. Here's how to set it up on the Epix Pro:

**Using Back to Start:**
1. From the watch face, press **START**
2. Select your activity (hiking, running, etc.)
3. Press **UP** or **MENU** during the activity
4. Navigate to **Navigation → Back to Start**
5. Choose either **Straight Line** or **Trace Back** (retraces your exact route)

**The start location is set automatically** the moment you press START on your activity — wherever you are standing is locked in as "start."

**If you want to pre-set a location before leaving:**
1. Go to **Menu → Navigation → Saved Locations**
2. Save your current location (camp, trailhead, car, etc.) as a waypoint
3. Later, use **Navigation → Saved Locations** → select it → **Navigate To**

**Pro tip — Trace Back vs Straight Line:**
- **Trace Back** is safer in terrain — it follows the exact path you walked in reverse
- **Straight Line** just points a bearing toward start, which may go through obstacles

The waypoint save method is the most reliable if you're heading somewhere unfamiliar and want a guaranteed return target before you start moving.
