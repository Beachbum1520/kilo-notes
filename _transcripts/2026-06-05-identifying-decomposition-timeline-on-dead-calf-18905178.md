So "puffy" is ambiguous in both directions. If she's still springing — udder filling, vulva slack and swollen, maybe some edema — that's a cow getting *ready* to calve, and this carcass isn't hers. But a cow who calved 2-3 weeks back and is being kept milked by a freeloader calf can also look full and puffy. The udder alone won't separate those two stories.

What's more reliable at this stage:

**Vulva and discharge, not the bag.** A cow that calved a couple weeks ago may still show some lochia or a slack, recently-stretched vulva even if the bag is confusing you. A cow that hasn't calved yet won't have that post-calving look. That's a cleaner tell than the udder.

**Check whether a calf is actually drawing her down.** Pen her off or watch her a bit. If she's full in the morning and drawn down by evening with no calf of her own visible, something's nursing her — and you've got a calf-sharing situation to untangle. If she stays tight and full with nobody working her, she's pre-calving.

**Then the head count again,** because the Dexter-will-nurse-anything thing means a mis-mothered or stolen calf can hide a loss for weeks. You can't assume one carcass equals one missing calf if calves are swapping freely.

Honestly the carcass age is the one hard fact you've got. Everything cow-side is soft data with this breed. I'd trust the 2-3 weeks, treat the puffy cow as unconfirmed, and let the head count be the tiebreaker. If the numbers come up short by one, you've got your answer regardless of which cow it was.
