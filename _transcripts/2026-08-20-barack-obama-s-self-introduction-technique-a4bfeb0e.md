I couldn't pull the actual video content (LinkedIn blocks fetching), so I can't tell you exactly what Obama does in that clip. But here's the technique that's usually being pointed to in "watch how he introduces himself" clips like this — and how you could adapt it:

**The common structure:**
1. **Name + one line of context** — no long résumé recitation up front.
2. **Reframe status, don't announce it** — instead of listing titles, he'll often say what he *does* in plain terms ("I try to help people..." / "I spend my time on...") rather than "I am the [title] of [org]."
3. **Anchor to the room** — a quick nod to why he's speaking to *this* audience specifically, not a generic bio.
4. **Self-deprecating or humanizing beat** — a small moment of humor or humility that lowers the temperature before he gets serious.
5. **Land on the stakes** — close the intro by pivoting to why this moment/topic matters, which sets up everything that follows.

**How you could use this for something like the Spectrum SE/TSC kickoff or a GRE&T proposal call:**

Instead of: *"I'm Scott Watts, Senior Director of Hospitality Operations at Blueprint RF, and I've been in hospitality technology for 24 years..."*

Try: *"I'm Scott — I run the team that makes sure the Wi-Fi actually works in the room you're sleeping in tonight. I've been doing this since 2002, back when 'guest Wi-Fi' meant a router taped under the front desk. [beat] Today I want to walk you through how we get from a signed contract to a guest who never thinks about their internet at all — which, if we do it right, is the whole point."*

That's name → plain-language function → light humor/credibility → pivot to relevance for the room.
