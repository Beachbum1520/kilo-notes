### CLAUDE (2026-08-24T18:20)
Generally, yes — you can drop or reduce supplemental life insurance at any time during the year, not just at open enrollment. That's different from health/dental/vision.

The reason: medical, dental, and vision elections are usually run through a Section 125 cafeteria plan, which is what locks you into your election until the next open enrollment unless you have an IRS-recognized qualifying life event (marriage, birth, divorce, etc.). Supplemental life insurance premiums are typically **not** pre-tax under that same cafeteria plan structure, so the IRS restriction that locks in your health elections generally doesn't apply to life insurance. Most employers let you decrease or cancel supplemental life coverage anytime via a simple form, effective the next pay period or the following month.

The asymmetry that matters here: canceling/decreasing is usually easy any time; **increasing** coverage later is the side that gets hard — that typically requires medical underwriting (evidence of insurability) even outside open enrollment, and you could be declined or rated up based on health at that point.

So the sequencing you're describing works in your favor: take the $950K now as your floor, shop the individual term market without time pressure, and if you find better/cheaper coverage, cancel or reduce the Charter supplemental election once the new policy is in force — don't cancel until the replacement is actually approved and active, so you're never uninsured in the gap.

One thing to verify directly rather than assume: confirm with Charter's HR/benefits portal or Securian directly that (a) mid-year cancellation is allowed for this specific plan and (b) whether cancellation takes effect immediately or after a notice period. Policies vary enough between carriers/employers that this is worth a two-minute confirmation before you rely on it, especially since you're navigating open enrollment for a first year at a new company post-merger and their specific plan documents govern, not general norms.
