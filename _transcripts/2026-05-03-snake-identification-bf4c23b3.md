### CLAUDE (2026-05-03T15:58)
 That's an **Eastern Garter Snake** (*Thamnophis sirtalis sirtalis*) — completely harmless and actually beneficial to have around the farm.

Key ID features visible in your photos:
- Three light yellowish stripes running down a dark brown/black body (one mid-back, two on the lower sides)
- Checkered dark pattern between the stripes
- Round pupils, no heat pits, and a slender body without the heavy build of a viper
- The head is only slightly wider than the neck

Garter snakes are non-venomous and pose no real threat to people, dogs, or your livestock. They eat frogs, toads, slugs, earthworms, small rodents, and the occasional small fish — so they're free pest control. If grabbed they may musk (release a smelly fluid) or give a small nip, but there's no medical concern.

The only thing worth a quick mental note: in Georgia, the lookalikes you actually want to distinguish from this are the **ribbon snake** (similar but skinnier, with a longer tail and unmarked white lip — also harmless) and any juvenile water snake. None of those are venomous either. The venomous snakes you'd want to watch for in Franklin, GA — copperheads, cottonmouths, rattlesnakes — all have triangular heads, vertical pupils, and stout bodies, none of which this snake has.

Safe to leave alone, or relocate with the snake hook if it's somewhere inconvenient.
