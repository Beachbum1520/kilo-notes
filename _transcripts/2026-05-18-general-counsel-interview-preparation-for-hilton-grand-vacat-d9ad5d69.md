### CLAUDE (2026-05-18T19:59)
 This is a smart question to ask — it signals she's evaluating them, not just hoping to get picked. The trick is framing it as due diligence, not suspicion. Asking about a long vacancy is legitimate; you want to understand whether it's a hiring bar issue, a scope/comp mismatch, internal reorg churn, or something about the role itself that didn't work for prior candidates.

A few angles she can choose from depending on tone:

**Straightforward / professional:**
"I noticed the role has been open for a while. Can you share what's made finding the right fit challenging, and what's changed in how you're thinking about the position since it was first posted?"

**More curious / collaborative:**
"I'd love to understand the history of this search. Has the scope or profile evolved since it opened in December? That would help me understand where you've landed on what you're really looking for."

**Direct but soft:**
"One thing I'd like to ask candidly — the role has been open for several months. What's been the gap between the candidates you've seen and what you need? I want to make sure I'm addressing that directly."

A couple of things worth flagging for her:

The answer matters as much as the question. If they say "we're being selective for the right fit," that's normal for GC roles. If they describe scope changes, reporting line changes, or two failed hires, that's a signal about organizational clarity and what she'd be walking into.

For a GC role specifically, she should also probe *why now* — is this a newly created position, a replacement, or a re-scoped role after someone left? And who does it report to (CEO, CLO, GC of parent)? At HGV's size and complexity (timeshare, public company, lots of consumer regulatory exposure), the reporting line tells her a lot about the seat's real authority.

Tell her congrats and good luck — that's a real role.
