### SCOTT (2026-05-10T19:46)
What is SLAM?

### CLAUDE (2026-05-10T19:46)
 **SLAM = Shock, Level, And Maintain**

It's a methodology developed by the Trouble Free Pool (TFP) community for killing algae and clearing green/cloudy pools. It's not a product — it's a *protocol*. The key insight is that "shocking" isn't a one-time dump of chemicals; it's a sustained state you hold the pool in until the algae is completely dead.

**The three parts:**

1. **Shock** — Raise free chlorine (FC) to shock level for your CYA
2. **Level** — Maintain that FC level continuously, testing and redosing multiple times per day
3. **And Maintain** — Keep at shock level until you pass all three exit criteria

**Shock level is determined by your CYA**, not a fixed number. This is what most pool stores get wrong. The FC/CYA ratio is what matters because CYA binds chlorine and reduces its killing power:

| CYA | Normal FC target | Shock (SLAM) FC |
|-----|------------------|-----------------|
| 30  | 4-6              | 12              |
| 40  | 5-7              | 16              |
| 50  | 6-8              | 20              |
| 60  | 7-9              | 24              |
| 80  | 9-11             | 31              |
| 100 | 11-13            | 39              |

This is why CYA testing is critical before SLAMing — without it, you're guessing at the dose.

**Three exit criteria — must pass ALL THREE to end SLAM:**

1. **Overnight Chlorine Loss Test (OCLT):** Test FC at sunset, again at sunrise before sun hits pool. Loss of ≤1 ppm = passing. Algae consumes chlorine in the dark; if FC drops more than 1 ppm overnight, algae is still alive.
2. **CC (Combined Chloramines) ≤ 0.5 ppm:** CC is the byproduct of chlorine killing organic matter. High CC means chlorine is still working on stuff.
3. **Water is clear** — you can see the bottom drain clearly.

**Why SLAM works when "shocking" often doesn't:**

Pool store advice usually says "add a bag of shock" — one dose, then wait. Problem is, algae regrows fast and consumes the chlorine before it kills everything. By morning, FC is back to zero and algae is winning again. SLAM holds FC at lethal levels *continuously* for days, which is what's actually required to kill all algae cells, oxidize their corpses, and clear the water.

**Practical execution:**

- Test FC every 2-4 hours during daylight, dose back up to shock level each time
- Brush walls/floor 1-2x daily to dislodge algae biofilm
- Run pump 24/7
- Clean filter when pressure rises 25% above clean baseline
- Keep pH at 7.2-7.5 (lower end — high pH reduces chlorine effectiveness)
- Don't add anything else (no algaecide, no clarifier, no phosphate remover) — they're unnecessary and many interfere with the process

**The TFP resource** (troublefreepool.com) has free SLAM guides and a Pool Math calculator that does all dosing automatically based on your test results. Worth bookmarking — it'll handle the chlorine math, CYA targets, and acid dosing for you all season.

Your golf swing analogy fits perfectly here: SLAM is small adjustments based on measurements, repeated until the pool tells you it's done. Not one big swing and hope.

 **No, not daily. Backwash by pressure, not by schedule.**

Backwashing too often is one of the most common SLAM mistakes. Here's why and when to actually do it.

**Why daily backwashing hurts you:**

1. **A slightly dirty sand bed filters better than a clean one.** The trapped debris creates smaller passages that catch finer particles. A freshly backwashed filter lets fine dead algae pass right through.
2. **You lose 200-400 gallons of treated water per backwash.** Every backwash drops your pool level, dumps chlorinated water, and forces you to add fresh water (which dilutes chemistry and may add iron/metals from your well).
3. **You're working against yourself.** Pool clears slower because the filter is less efficient after each backwash.

**The right rule: backwash when pressure rises 25% above clean baseline.**

- Note your clean starting pressure right now (probably 10-15 PSI on a typical residential system)
- Backwash when gauge reads 25% higher (e.g., 12 PSI clean → backwash at 15 PSI)
- During heavy SLAM, this might be every 1-2 days
- Once water clears, might be every 1-2 weeks

**Backwash procedure (sand filter):**

1. Pump OFF
2. Multiport valve to BACKWASH
3. Pump ON, watch sight glass
4. Run until sight glass shows clear water — typically 2-4 minutes
5. Pump OFF
6. Valve to RINSE
7. Pump ON for 30-60 seconds (resettles the sand bed)
8. Pump OFF
9. Valve to FILTER
10. Pump ON, note new pressure (this is your new "clean" baseline)

**Top off water after each backwash** to keep skimmers primed. Add chlorine to compensate for what you dumped.

**Special considerations for your situation:**

- **Cover is still on through Thursday** — you can't see the water clearing or judge by visual cues, only by pressure gauge

What’s SLAM and SWG

### CLAUDE (2026-05-11T14:38)
 You asked about SLAM earlier — here's both, quick reference:

**SLAM = Shock, Level, And Maintain**

The methodology for killing algae and clearing a green pool. Not a product — a process. You raise free chlorine to a "shock level" determined by your CYA, then *hold it there continuously* (testing and redosing multiple times daily) until you pass three exit criteria: overnight chlorine loss ≤1 ppm, combined chloramines ≤0.5 ppm, and water visually clear. Typically takes 3-7 days for a green pool.

The key difference from "shocking" is the *sustained* part. Most pool store advice says dump a bag and wait — that fails because algae regrows faster than one dose can kill it all. SLAM holds lethal chlorine levels constant for days, which is what actually kills the algae completely.

**SWG = Salt Water Generator** (also called salt chlorine generator, salt cell, salt system)

That's your CircuPool unit. It's the device that takes dissolved salt in your pool water and uses electrolysis to generate chlorine continuously. Salt water flows through the cell, electrical current splits NaCl into sodium and chlorine, and the chlorine sanitizes the pool. The salt itself isn't consumed — it cycles back when chlorine combines with organics, so you only add salt to replace what's lost to splash-out, backwashing, and rain dilution.

SWG advantages: no manual chlorine adding, no chloramine smell, gentler on skin/eyes, no CYA-adding products needed.

SWG requirements: salinity in the 3000-4000 ppm range (your unit), water temp above ~60°F to generate efficiently, clean cell, balanced water chemistry.

**Why both matter for your situation:**

Right now you're doing a **manual SLAM with liquid chlorine** because the SWG can't keep up with a green pool's chlorine demand. Once water is clear and chemistry is dialed in, you transition back to the **SWG** for normal season operation — it makes chlorine daily as the pump runs, you just monitor and adjust output percentage.

The SLAM clears the pool. The SWG maintains it.
