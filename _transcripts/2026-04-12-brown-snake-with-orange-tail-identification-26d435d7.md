Based on brown body + orange tail in the Franklin, GA area, here are the most likely candidates:

---

**Most likely: Copperhead juvenile**
Young copperheads have a **bright yellow-green to orange tail tip** used to lure prey. Body is brown with hourglass banding. **Venomous — treat with caution.** Very common on farms in Heard County.

---

**Also possible (non-venomous):**

- **Worm Snake** — brown on top with a pink or orange underside, reaches up to 14 inches, very secretive, found in leaf litter and loose soil. Harmless.

- **Red-bellied Snake** — dorsal color ranges from fawn to black, often with parallel stripes; belly is bright red to orange. Small, harmless.

- **Plain-bellied Water Snake** — adults have a gray-brown dorsal surface with a bright red to yellow belly. Non-venomous but can be aggressive.

---

**Key ID test for the copperhead:**
- Triangular/arrow-shaped head wider than the neck
- Hourglass-shaped brown bands on the body
- Chunky, heavy-bodied build
- Vertical slit pupils (if close enough to see safely)

**Bottom line for the farm:** If it had a distinct triangular head and hourglass markings, treat it as a copperhead and give it wide berth. If it was small (under 12–14 inches), slender, and the orange was on the belly rather than just the tail tip, likely a harmless worm or red-bellied snake.
