**Try these in order — stop as soon as it's clean:**

1. **Garden hose, high pressure, straight down through the plates.** Iron deposits this fresh often blast right off. Do this first every time.
2. **Plastic or wood shim** (a paint stir stick works) between plates for anything stubborn. Never metal — the coating is a thin ruthenium/iridium oxide layer on titanium, and scratching it is permanent capacity loss.
3. **Acid soak only if 1 and 2 fail.** 4:1 water to acid, and *acid into water*, never the reverse. Cell upright with the bottom capped (CircuPool sells a cleaning stand; a rubber test plug or the cell union with a cap works). Fill only to cover the plates — keep it well away from the cord/electronics end. Soak 10–15 minutes max, or until the fizzing stops, whichever comes first. Rinse hard with the hose.

Every acid wash costs you cell life. Two or three a season is fine; monthly will eat it.

**Gentler option worth trying for iron specifically:** a 50/50 white vinegar soak for 30–60 minutes. Slower, but far kinder to the coating, and iron oxide gives up to vinegar more readily than calcium does.

The real fix is upstream — this is the same iron that caused your July events. Every trickle fill deposits a little more here. If the auto-leveler happens, cell fouling should drop off with it.

**Setup**
- Gloves, eye protection, outdoors, stand upwind. 
- Cap the bottom end — cleaning stand, rubber test plug, or a union with a cap. Cell vertical, **cord end up**, always.
- Mix in a plastic bucket first, then pour into the cell. Never mix inside the cell.

**Solution**
- 4 parts water to 1 part muriatic acid. **Water first, acid into it.**
- Mix enough to cover the plates and nothing more. Acid touching the cord end or sensor is how cells die early.

**Soak**
- Pour in slowly. It'll fizz immediately.
- **Watch the fizz, not the clock.** When it stops or goes to a lazy trickle, the acid is done working on deposit and has started working on your coating. Dump it.
- Hard ceiling of 15 minutes even if it's still bubbling. If it needs more, dump, rinse, mix fresh, and do a second short soak — fresh acid on a partly-cleaned cell beats a long soak in spent acid.

**After**
- Rinse hard with the hose, inside and out, a good 30 seconds. Any acid left will keep eating.
- Neutralize the spent solution with baking soda until it stops fizzing, then dilute heavily before dumping. Don't pour it on the lawn or near the equipment pad.

**Then look at the plates.** Clean coating is matte black. If you see dull silver-gray patches, that's bare titanium showing through — coating loss, and that cell is on borrowed time. Worth knowing before you're mid-trip and Angie's watching FC slide.

Reinstall with the flow arrow correct and check the union o-rings while it's apart.
