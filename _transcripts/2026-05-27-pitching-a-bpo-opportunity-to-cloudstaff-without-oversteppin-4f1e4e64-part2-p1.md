## The basic architecture

The core question in any equity-partner structure is: **what's the vehicle, and what does each side put into it?**

Three common shapes, and the differences matter:

**Shape A — Equity in a new subsidiary/vertical of Cloudstaff.** Cloudstaff spins up a hospitality vertical as a subsidiary or defined business unit. You get equity in that entity specifically — not in Cloudstaff parent. You'd typically get a founder-level stake in the vertical (10-30% range is the ballpark for someone bringing operational leadership and warm demand into a funded parent's infrastructure) with vesting over 3-4 years, an operating title (President or GM of the vertical), and comp for running it.

*Pros:* Cleanest alignment — your upside is tied directly to what you build. Your equity has a defined P&L behind it, which makes valuation events tractable (sale of the vertical, spin-out, etc.).
*Cons:* You're at the mercy of how Cloudstaff structures the vertical legally. If they later fold it back into parent or restructure, your stake can get diluted or converted on terms you don't control. Requires a well-drafted subsidiary agreement with anti-dilution and drag/tag provisions.

**Shape B — Equity in Cloudstaff parent.** You come in as a senior operator with a stake in the whole company. Typically much smaller percentage (1-5% is generous for a senior hire into a funded, post-VC-round company at your stage), often via options or RSUs on a standard vesting schedule.

*Pros:* You're aligned with the whole company; if Cloudstaff has a broader exit, you participate. Simpler structure, standard docs.
*Cons:* Your equity gets diluted by everything the parent does — future rounds, acquisitions, other verticals. Your operational contribution to hospitality gets diffused into the parent's overall performance. You could crush it in hospitality and see modest returns because parent performance dragged.

**Shape C — Joint venture / separate legal entity.** Cloudstaff and you each hold equity in a separate LLC or corporation created specifically for the hospitality BPO business. Cloudstaff contributes capital, infrastructure, WFM tooling, and the Manila entity/legal-employer plumbing. You contribute the operating leadership, playbook, industry relationships, and demand pipeline.

*Pros:* Cleanest separation of interests. Your equity is in a clearly-defined entity with its own P&L, board, and exit potential. Cloudstaff doesn't get to fold it back into parent unilaterally.
*Cons:* Most complex to set up. Requires real legal work upfront and negotiation over control, governance, and eventual exit rights. Cloudstaff may resist because it's less integrated into their story for their own investors.

## What a "cliff" means in vesting

Standard equity vesting works over time — typically 4 years. A "cliff" is a period at the beginning where you get *nothing* if you leave. Then it unlocks in a chunk.

Standard structure: **4-year vest, 1-year cliff.** That means:
- If you leave in month 11: you get 0% of your equity. Nothing.
- If you make it to month 12: 25% vests all at once (the "cliff" hits).
- After that: the remaining 75% vests monthly (or quarterly) over the next 3 years.
