### CLAUDE (2026-05-22T10:02)
 Great shot already — the timing and light are solid. Here's how to level it up:

**More water**
- Submerge fully, then come up fast with head tilted further back — the deeper the dunk, the more water mass follows
- Keep arms sweeping outward underwater as you rise to push a wall of water up with you
- Shoot in shallower water (thigh-deep) so the bottom splash kicks up too
- Golden hour light makes water droplets catch fire — this midday light is decent but morning/late afternoon is better

**Bigger hair flip arc**
- Start with head tucked chin-to-chest underwater, then whip back hard in one explosive motion — the tighter the coil, the bigger the arc
- Longer hair = bigger arc obviously, but a leave-in conditioner before the shoot helps hair stay together as one dramatic sweep rather than splitting apart
- The flip in your photo splits mid-arc — one hard fast whip beats a slow controlled one every time

**Camera/angle**
- Shoot at 240fps (slow motion) then speed-ramp: full slo-mo on the flip peak, then speed up as the water falls
- Position camera at water level or just below — this shot is slightly above, which flattens the drama
- Shoot slightly into the sun (backlit) so every droplet glows

**The shot you want** is basically this photo but 3x more explosive — it's a timing/execution issue more than a setup issue. The location is perfect.
