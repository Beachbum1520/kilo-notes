# Claude project memory: Concrete Work

**Purpose & context**

Scott is a livestock farmer in west Georgia (LaGrange–Columbus area, near GA-219) running a cattle operation. He's experienced, hands-on, and works mostly solo. He prefers direct answers that lead with the number or recommendation first, then the reasoning — and pushes back when Claude over-explains, makes wrong assumptions, or gets ahead of him on a project before he's finished describing it.

**Current state**

Scott has successfully completed two Tusker 354NG energy-free livestock waterer pads (65"×60", 5.5" thick, poured on tamped bare red clay using 2x6 forms). Key specs for reference:
- Riser pipe: 6" OD, centered left-to-right (29.25" from each side), 15.25" from top edge, 38.25" from bottom
- Mix: 60 lb bags of Sakrete/Quikrete High-Strength (~28 bags per pad, 56 total); mixed in a rented Sunbelt 9 cu ft gas tow-behind drum mixer
- Actual mixer capacity: ~4.5 bags per batch (~6 batches per pad), not Claude's initial conservative 2-bag estimate
- Forms used screws for clean reuse; broom-finished center with smooth border; forms left on post-pour

Two projects are in active planning:

**1. Well/pump pad expansion:** 5'×8' → 7'×10' (a 30 sq ft band at 3.5" thick to match the existing 2x4-formed slab)

**2. Chute pad expansion + pole barn:**
- Pad: 5'×11' → 16'×20'; extension is a human working area (wire mesh lifted to mid-slab, not rebar mat); #4 dowels at the seam to existing pad; poly vapor barrier underneath (covered area)
- Both pads (well and chute) to be poured same day from one ready-mix truck (~3.5 yards, 4,000 psi with fiber mesh, 3.5" thick); truck can reach both locations
- Barn: 16'×20', 6 posts (three per side, 0-10-20 spacing, 10' bays), 12' eave height; 6×6 UC4B ground-contact pressure-treated posts, 16' length
- Posts must be set and cured before the slab truck arrives
- Post-setting method: dry-pour with forced water in lifts (practical given no mixer on hand without rental)
- Auger bit size not yet confirmed — bag count per post hole is pending that measurement

**On the horizon**

- Confirm tractor auger bit diameter to finalize post-hole concrete bag count
- Sequence and schedule: post-setting → cure → ready-mix truck for both pads on same day

**Key learnings & principles**

- Scott catches errors precisely — confirmed pipe centerline was centered (not offset), caught incorrect post-spacing math that produced an unwanted 20' barn length, and pushed back on Claude getting ahead on a two-part project
- Don't assume; wait for Scott to finish describing a project before proposing solutions
- Lead with the number or direct recommendation, then explain
- His red clay soil (bare, tamped) has performed fine as a base without gravel or plastic for these applications
