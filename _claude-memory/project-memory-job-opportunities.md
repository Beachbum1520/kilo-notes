**Key learnings & principles**

- **Don't invert the buyer/seller dynamic with Lloyd.** Scott is the asset being courted; behaving like a supplicant undermines his position. He initiated the question about building a hospitality vertical, but the ask flows to Lloyd, not from him.
- **Nothing strategic in writing while employed.** A standing discipline—no commitments, no detailed plans, no paper trail that could complicate his position at Charter or with any future employer.
- **Treat silence as data, not emergency.** Jay and Macon's three weeks of silence after Lloyd's introduction was correctly read as disorganization, not disinterest—confirmed by the unsolicited dinner outreach. Scott does not chase.
- **The call center is the moat.** In hospitality WiFi managed services, all providers sell the same gear to the same brand standards. The differentiator is sustained investment in training people. CallTek's dominance was built on this discipline; its current vulnerability is the erosion of it.
- **Accountability without authority doesn't work structurally.** A consistent position Scott holds and flags proactively when scope expansion discussions lack matching authority.
- **Forfeiture math shapes negotiating posture.** The cost-of-leaving framework (by departure timing) is a live tool for any external conversations with Lloyd or others.
- **Corrections are immediate and specific.** Scott flags factual errors about his background, tenure, or role in real time and expects them to be corrected without defensiveness.
